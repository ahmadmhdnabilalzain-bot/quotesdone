# Conversation playbook: HVAC, plumbing and MEP

**Categories:** hvac, plumbing, mep, combined-mep, insulation, water-tank-cleaning

General rules for this group:
- Find out first: emirate, property type (villa, apartment, office, shop, warehouse), owner or tenant, and whether the building has central/district cooling.
- Apartment and community jobs: building management or the master developer gives the NOC, working hours and where outdoor units may go. Tenants need landlord approval.
- Utility approvals: DEWA (Dubai), ADDC/AADC (Abu Dhabi), SEWA (Sharjah), EtihadWE (northern emirates). New connections, load increases and meter changes go through them; the supplier's licensed contractor applies.
- Dubai designs are checked against the Dubai Building Code; Trakhees in PCFC zones; DM/DDA/municipality for permits. Do not quote clause numbers.
- No figures or prices; ask for the budget tier only.

## HVAC (hvac)
Covers split, ducted, package, VRF/VRV, chilled-water FCUs, ventilation, AC repair, AMC and duct cleaning.

### AC replacement (most common request; go deep)
- **Open:** How many units are you replacing, and what type are they now (wall split, ducted/concealed, package, cassette, window, VRF)? Is the old unit dead, or are you upgrading?
- **Must-haves (asked in this order, one or two a turn, then the summary):** size: the room size and sun (or the area and rooms for a whole home); existing: what is there now (type and age) or a new installation; system: the type wanted (split, ducted) and inverter; services: where the outdoor unit and pipes go, or the ceiling void for ducts; location; timing
- **Then ask (in order, one or two at a time):**
  1. **Cooling source:** own AC units, or central/district chiller with FCUs (then it is FCU/thermostat work, not new condensers; building management controls the chilled water).
  2. **Rooms served:** room use and size per unit (length x width, ceiling height); bedroom, majlis, living, kitchen, office.
  3. **Exposure:** west/south-facing, large glass, top floor or roof under the room, number of occupants, kitchen heat, server equipment. These push capacity up.
  4. **Current capacity:** nameplate tonnage/BTU of the old unit (photo of the indoor and outdoor nameplate). Was it cooling well? If not, capacity or airflow may be wrong, not just the unit.
  5. **Unit type choice:**
     - Wall split: one room, simplest, fewest works.
     - Ducted/concealed split: false ceiling needed; supply/return grilles; check existing ducts and ceiling access panels.
     - Cassette: open-plan or shop ceilings.
     - Package unit: roof/ground unit, larger villas and shops; check roof curb and crane access.
     - VRF/VRV: many indoor units on one outdoor system; for whole villas/offices; needs design and engineer layout.
  6. **Like-for-like or change?** Same type and size in the same location is the fastest. Changing type means new pipes, ceiling work, electrical changes.
  7. **Pipework and drain reuse:** existing copper size and condition; old pipes sized for the old refrigerant may not suit the new one; flushing or new pipes. Is the condensate drain working and falling correctly? Concealed pipes in walls need inspection before reuse.
  8. **Outdoor unit location:** current position (balcony, roof, ledge, ground, plant room); distance and height difference to indoor unit; is the location shaded and ventilated? Building rules may fix the location and cover style; ask building management.
  9. **Electrical:** existing isolator and breaker; single or three phase; larger units may need a cable upgrade; any load increase goes via DEWA/ADDC/SEWA through a licensed contractor.
  10. **Refrigerant:** customer may have an older unit on a phased-out refrigerant; new units use current refrigerants (UAE is phasing down HFCs under federal rules). Recovery of old refrigerant by the technician, not venting.
  11. **Efficiency label:** UAE units carry a 1 to 5 star efficiency label (MoIAT); ask the star rating wanted.
  12. **Removal of old unit:** dismantle, take away, disposal; customer wants to keep the old unit?
  13. **Access:** floor level, lift, crane for package units, ceiling access for ducted.
  14. **Controls:** thermostat type, Wi-Fi/smart control, zoning.
  15. **Extras:** duct cleaning or new ducts, new grilles, insulation repair, AMC after install, warranty terms.
  16. Timeline (summer urgency), working hours, NOC.
- **Photo/drawing tells you:** unit type, brand plate and capacity, outdoor location, visible pipe and insulation condition, drain route. **It cannot tell you:** refrigerant leaks, duct leakage, concealed pipe condition, electrical capacity, real heat load. A supplier's site survey (and a heat-load calc for larger or VRF jobs) decides final sizing; DEWA/ADDC/SEWA decides any load increase; building management decides the outdoor location.
- **Misconceptions to correct:**
  - Bigger is not better; oversized units short-cycle and leave the room humid.
  - A unit that "does not cool" may have a gas leak, dirty coil, blocked filter or leaky duct; repair may beat replacement.
  - Reusing old pipes is not always possible with a different refrigerant or capacity.
  - Inverter units save running energy but still need correct sizing.
  - Undersizing is the commoner mistake: a 1-ton (12,000 BTU/h) split suits a small bedroom. In UAE heat allow roughly 1 ton per 10-14 m2 of ordinary room, more for west or south sun, a top floor or large glazing; a 28 m2 sunny living room is about 2 to 2.5 tons. The supplier's heat-load check fixes the size; an undersized unit runs flat out and never reaches the set temperature. When a customer names a capacity that is clearly too small, say so first. (Ledger 1200 scenario bank, 27 Oct 2026.)
  - Outdoor units in enclosed, unshaded spots lose capacity in summer heat.
- **Complete quotable request (replacement) =** emirate, property type, floor; number of units; per unit: room use, size, exposure, existing type/capacity, new type, capacity if known, star rating; pipe and drain reuse or new; outdoor unit location and distance; electrical supply; removal and disposal; access/crane; controls; NOC/working hours; start date.

### Other HVAC work
- **Open:** New installation, repair, maintenance contract, or duct cleaning?
- **Then ask:** number and type of units; symptoms (no cooling, water leak, noise, smell, high bills); age of units; for AMC: number of units, visit frequency wanted, parts included or not; for duct cleaning: number of units/ducts, villa or apartment, include coil cleaning and disinfection; fresh-air or exhaust fans needed.
- **Price drivers:** unit count and type, capacity, new pipes and ducts, false-ceiling openings, electrical works, crane/height, refrigerant top-up/recovery, spare parts, AMC scope and frequency, after-hours work, permits.
- **Complete quotable request =** emirate, property type; job type; unit list (type, capacity, count, age); symptoms or scope; access; working hours; start date.

## Plumbing (plumbing)
- **Open:** What is the problem or job: leak, blockage, low pressure, no hot water, new fixtures, or full bathroom/kitchen replumbing?
- **Must-haves (asked in this order, one or two a turn, then the summary):** scope: the problem or the job; size: how many fixtures, or the size or capacity; existing: what is there now and its condition; layout: the fixtures and where they go; location; timing
- **Then ask (in order, one or two at a time):**
  - Where is it: bathroom, kitchen, roof tank, pump room, outside? Villa or apartment?
  - For leaks: visible water, wet wall or ceiling below, high water bill? Neighbour's ceiling affected?
  - Pipe material if known (PPR, CPVC, PEX, copper, GI in older villas).
  - Fixtures: WC type (floor/wall-hung with concealed cistern), mixers, showers, heater type and size (electric storage, instant, solar), water pump and booster, filter/softener.
  - Customer supplies fixtures or supplier supplies?
  - Chasing walls and retiling needed after pipe replacement?
  - Drainage: blocked floor drain, gully, manhole, sewer line; any smell?
  - Urgency: emergency today or scheduled.
- **Photo/drawing tells you:** fixture type, visible leak point, stains, heater label. **It cannot tell you:** hidden pipe leak location, drain blockage depth, pipe condition. Leak detection (thermal or acoustic) is done on site.
- **Price drivers:** emergency call-out, leak detection, pipe material and length, concealed vs exposed, wall chasing and making good (tiles), fixture grade and who supplies, heater/pump size, drain jetting/CCTV, access to roof tanks, building NOC, after-hours.
- **Misconceptions to correct:** a ceiling stain often comes from the flat above, not your pipe; replacing one fitting rarely fixes old GI pipework; water heaters in UAE summer may need a mixing valve, not a bigger heater.
- **Complete quotable request =** emirate, property type; job type; location; symptoms; pipe material; fixture list and who supplies; tiles/making good included; urgency; access; NOC; start date.

## MEP and combined MEP (mep, combined-mep)
Single trade (mep) or one contractor for mechanical, electrical and plumbing together (combined-mep), mostly fit-outs, extensions and new builds.
- **Open:** Is this a fit-out, villa extension, new build, or modification? Do you have approved drawings or a consultant?
- **Must-haves (asked in this order, one or two a turn, then the summary):** size: the building size and use; scope: the systems in scope; system: the maintenance terms (planned visits, emergency response) or the installation type; design: drawings or a consultant; location; timing
- **Then ask (in order, one or two at a time):**
  - Area (m2), use (office, retail, restaurant, clinic, villa), floors.
  - Which systems: HVAC, power and lighting, plumbing and drainage, fire fighting/alarm (civil defence), low current, gas.
  - Design: drawings exist, or design-and-build needed? Who is the consultant of record?
  - Base build: shell and core provisions (chilled water, power load, drainage points).
  - Approvals needed: DM/DDA/Trakhees/municipality permit, DEWA/ADDC/SEWA load and connection, Civil Defence (DCD in Dubai) for fire systems, landlord/mall NOC.
  - Testing and commissioning, as-built drawings, handover documents.
  - Timeline, working hours (malls often night-only).
- **Photo/drawing tells you:** layout, scope size, existing services. **It cannot tell you:** base-build capacity, authority comments. Drawings plus the landlord's fit-out guide decide scope; the authorities above decide approval.
- **Price drivers:** design or build-only, system count, equipment grades, authority submissions and inspections, night shifts, testing and commissioning, as-builts, site logistics.
- **Misconceptions to correct:** a fit-out cannot start MEP works before the permit and landlord NOC; fire systems need a Civil Defence-approved contractor.
- **Complete quotable request =** emirate, site type and area; scope by system; drawings available or design needed; consultant; base-build provisions; approvals required; T&C and handover; working hours; start date.

## Insulation (insulation)
- **Open:** What do you want insulated: roof, walls, AC ducts, chilled-water or refrigerant pipes, water tanks, or soundproofing?
- **Must-haves (asked in this order, one or two a turn, then the summary):** scope: what is insulated; size; existing; location; timing
- **Then ask:** area (m2) or pipe/duct length and size; new or replacing damaged insulation; reason (heat gain, condensation drips, noise, energy bills); material preference (foam board, spray foam, mineral wool, rubber/closed-cell pipe insulation, reflective foil); roof waterproofing combined?; access (roof, ceiling void); fire rating requirement for commercial; green-building requirement (Al Sa'fat in Dubai, Estidama in Abu Dhabi) on new projects.
- **Photo/drawing tells you:** current insulation condition, sweating pipes, roof type. **It cannot tell you:** thickness needed, hidden wet insulation. Supplier confirms on site.
- **Price drivers:** material type and thickness, area/length, removal of wet or old insulation, cladding/jacketing, height and access, roof waterproofing combination, fire-rating certificates.
- **Misconceptions to correct:** dripping AC ducts usually need insulation repair plus a vapour barrier, not just a drain fix; foil alone is not full insulation.
- **Complete quotable request =** emirate, property type; element; area or length and size; purpose; material preference; removal yes/no; access; certificates needed; start date.

## Water tank cleaning (water-tank-cleaning)
- **Open:** How many tanks, and where are they (roof, underground, both)? Villa, building or commercial?
- **Must-haves (asked in this order, one or two a turn, then the summary):** size: how many tanks and their capacity; scope: roof or underground tanks; location; timing
- **Then ask:** tank capacity (gallons or litres) and material (GRP, concrete, polyethylene, steel); last cleaning date; any complaint (smell, colour, sediment, insects); disinfection and lab water test wanted; certificate needed (municipality requirements for buildings and food outlets; ask the municipality or building management what they require); access (ladder, manhole size, confined space); water shut-off window and notice to residents; repairs (lid, ladder, float valve, cracks).
- **Photo/drawing tells you:** tank type, location, visible dirt, lid condition. **It cannot tell you:** inside condition or water quality; a lab test does.
- **Price drivers:** number, size and type of tanks; underground/confined-space access; disinfection; lab test and certificate; repairs; after-hours shutdown.
- **Misconceptions to correct:** a clear-looking tank can still fail a lab test; cleaning without disinfection does not make water safe.
- **Complete quotable request =** emirate, property type; tank count, capacity, material and location; last cleaning; complaints; disinfection and lab test; certificate needed; access; shutdown window; repairs; date.
