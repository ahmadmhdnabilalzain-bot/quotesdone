# Conversation playbook: Joinery and furniture

**Categories:** carpentry-joinery, custom-furniture, kitchens, upholstery, acrylic, job-shop-manufacturing

## Built-in joinery and custom furniture: wardrobes, vanities, TV units, doors, shelving (carpentry-joinery, custom-furniture)
- **Open:** "What is the piece, and which room?" then "Can you send a photo of the wall or space, plus the width, height and depth available?"
- **Then ask (in order, one or two at a time):**
  1. Exact dimensions: wall width, floor-to-ceiling height, usable depth; anything in the way (skirting, AC duct or grille, DB board, window, socket, column, sloped ceiling). Full height to the ceiling, or a gap/bulkhead above?
  2. Internal layout (wardrobe): split of long hanging (dresses/abayas, about 1600 mm clear), double hanging (shirts), shelves, drawers (how many, internal or external), shoe racks, pull-out trouser rail, mirror, safe box, internal lighting.
  3. Doors: hinged (soft-close), sliding (top-hung or bottom-rolling; needs about 600 mm+ depth), or open/walk-in. Opening: handles (style, length), handle-less J-profile, or push-to-open touch latch.
  4. Material: carcass (moisture-resistant MR MDF, plywood, particle board, solid wood); board thickness (commonly 18 mm). Finish: melamine/laminate, matt or high-gloss PU lacquer, veneer, acrylic sheet, glass or mirror fronts. Colour/texture code if known.
  5. Hardware class: soft-close hinges and runners, full-extension drawers; ask "standard or premium hardware?" (no brand names needed).
  6. Property: villa or apartment, floor, lift access, owned or rented; building/community working hours.
  7. Remove an existing unit? Wall patch/paint after? Sockets or lights to move (electrician)?
  8. Timeline (needed by when), budget range, number of rooms/units.
- **Photo/drawing tells you:** room type, rough proportions, the style the customer likes, existing finishes, visible obstructions. **It cannot tell you:** true dimensions, wall plumb/straightness, what is inside the wall (pipes, cables), the board quality in an inspiration photo, the internal layout the customer needs. Ask for tape measurements (W x H x D, mm or cm); final dimensions are fixed by a **site measure by the supplier** before production.
- **Price drivers (spec decisions):** size and full-height vs standard height; board type (MR MDF/ply vs particle board); finish (lacquer, veneer, acrylic take more labour than laminate); door system (sliding and mirror doors cost more than hinged); number of drawers and accessories; hardware grade; internal LED lighting (needs an electrician); installation height and access (lift booking in towers); removal and disposal of the old unit; landlord/building-management NOC in rented flats; restricted working hours in towers.
- **Misconceptions to correct:** "A photo is enough for an exact quote" (dimensions and the site measure decide). "Lacquer makes MDF waterproof" (only the faces; near wet areas use MR board with sealed edges). "Sliding doors save space everywhere" (they need extra depth and open only half the width at a time). "Ordinary MDF is fine in bathrooms or coastal flats" (humidity and AC condensation swell it; use MR board or ply). Solid wood moves with UAE heat and AC swings; engineered board is often more stable.
- **Complete quotable request =** item type and room; W x H x D; internal layout (hanging, shelf and drawer counts); door type and opening method; carcass material and finish/colour; hardware grade; extras (lighting, mirror, accessories); old unit removal yes/no; property type, area, access; NOC if rented; timeline; budget range.

### Founder's example: wardrobe for a studio, customer sends a photo
1. Confirm the wall in the photo is where it goes and ask W x H x D. Studios are often tight, so check there is about 600 mm depth for hanging.
2. Ask the split: long hanging, short hanging, shelves, drawers, shoes.
3. Ask doors: hinged, sliding (no swing space needed, which suits a small studio), or handle-less push-to-open.
4. Ask material and finish: white matt laminate, wood-grain, gloss lacquer, or mirror fronts to make the studio feel larger.
5. Ask hardware grade and any internal light.
6. Ask whether an AC unit, socket or switch is on that wall.
7. Ask whether the flat is rented or owned (building NOC), the floor and lift access.
8. Ask the timeline and budget.

Then read back a one-paragraph spec, have the customer confirm it, and send it to suppliers.

## Kitchens (kitchens)
- **Open:** "A new kitchen, or replacing an existing one?" then "Send a photo with the wall lengths, ceiling height, and where the sink, cooker and window are."
- **Then ask (in order, one or two at a time):**
  1. Layout: straight, L, U, galley, island/peninsula; wall lengths in mm; ceiling height; keep the sink/drain where it is or move it?
  2. Cabinets: base, wall, tall units; wall units to the ceiling? Drawers vs doors; corner solutions; pantry/tall larder.
  3. Carcass material (MR MDF, marine ply, particle board, aluminium or stainless) and door finish (laminate, matt/gloss PU lacquer, acrylic, veneer, solid wood, membrane).
  4. Worktop: quartz/engineered stone, granite, marble, solid surface, compact laminate, wood; thickness; edge profile; splashback (same stone, tile, glass).
  5. Appliances: supplied by the customer or by the supplier? Built-in oven/hob/hood/fridge/dishwasher sizes or model numbers (needed for cut-outs).
  6. Sink (undermount/inset, single/double) and tap; handles vs handle-less/push-to-open; hardware grade; internal accessories.
  7. Under-cabinet lighting, sockets; any gas line (LPG or piped gas changes need a licensed gas contractor and the gas provider's approval).
  8. Removal of the old kitchen, wall/floor tile repair; villa or apartment, access, NOC; timeline and budget.
- **Photo/drawing tells you:** layout type, window/door positions, existing plumbing location, style. **It cannot tell you:** wall lengths, out-of-square walls, pipe and drain routes, appliance sizes. Ask for measurements and appliance models; final sizes come from a **site measure by the supplier**.
- **Price drivers (spec decisions):** run length and unit count; carcass material; door finish; worktop material, thickness, number of cut-outs and joins; appliances in or out; accessories; moving plumbing, electrics or gas; removal/disposal; tower access and working hours; building NOC.
- **Misconceptions to correct:** "Kitchens are priced by the metre only" (worktop, appliances, accessories and finish change it a lot). "Marble is ideal for worktops" (it is porous and stains with lemon and oil; quartz needs less care). "Any carcass will do under the sink" (use MR or marine-grade board; leaks are common). Gas work is not joinery: it needs a licensed gas contractor.
- **Complete quotable request =** layout and wall lengths; ceiling height; unit counts and types; carcass and door finish; worktop material/thickness; splashback; appliances in/out with sizes; sink/tap; handles/opening; lighting; plumbing, electrical or gas changes; removal; property, location, access, NOC; timeline; budget.

## Upholstery (upholstery)
- **Open:** "Re-upholstering existing furniture, or new upholstered pieces?" then "A photo of each piece, and how many?"
- **Then ask (in order, one or two at a time):** piece type and count (sofa seats, dining chairs, headboard, cushions, wall panels, majlis seating); dimensions; fabric supplied by the customer or sourced by the supplier; fabric type (woven fabric, leatherette, genuine leather, velvet, outdoor UV-resistant) and colour; replace or keep the foam, and how firm; frame or spring repair?; details (piping, buttons/tufting, zips, removable covers); pickup/delivery or on-site work; timeline; budget.
- **Photo/drawing tells you:** shape, fabric condition, style. **It cannot tell you:** frame and spring condition, foam state, exact fabric metres. The supplier inspects at pickup and calculates the fabric quantity.
- **Price drivers (spec decisions):** fabric type and metres; foam replacement and grade; frame/spring repairs; tufting and piping; number of pieces; pickup/delivery or on-site work; outdoor-grade fabric for terraces.
- **Misconceptions to correct:** "Indoor fabric works outdoors" (UAE sun fades and rots it; use solution-dyed, UV-resistant outdoor fabric). Leatherette tends to crack and peel sooner in heat than genuine leather. Matching a pattern uses more fabric.
- **Complete quotable request =** piece list with counts and sizes; new or re-upholster; fabric source/type/colour; foam and frame work; details; indoor/outdoor; pickup/delivery; location; timeline; budget.

## Acrylic fabrication (acrylic)
- **Open:** "What is the item (display stand, sign panel, partition, box, shelf, cover)?" then "What size, and how many?"
- **Then ask (in order, one or two at a time):** dimensions and sheet thickness (e.g. 3, 5, 8, 10 mm or more); clear, frosted, coloured or mirror acrylic; laser/CNC cutting, edge finish (polished or flame), bending, bonding, engraving or printing; fixings (standoffs, brackets, hinges); indoor or outdoor (outdoors needs a UV-stable grade); artwork or drawing file (vector AI, DXF, PDF); installation needed?; quantity and repeat orders; timeline; budget.
- **Photo/drawing tells you:** shape, style, rough proportions. **It cannot tell you:** thickness, exact dimensions, load. Ask for a dimensioned drawing or vector file; the supplier confirms it can be built.
- **Price drivers (spec decisions):** sheet thickness and area; special colours or mirror sheet; cutting complexity; polishing, bending, bonding; printing or engraving; fixings; quantity; installation and working height.
- **Misconceptions to correct:** "Acrylic is unbreakable" (that is polycarbonate; acrylic is clearer but can crack). Thin acrylic sags over long spans and bows in outdoor heat. Signs on a building facade need a municipality permit and are handled through the signage trade.
- **Complete quotable request =** item; dimensions and thickness; sheet type/colour; processing (cut, polish, bend, print, engrave); fixings; indoor/outdoor; artwork file; quantity; install yes/no; location; timeline; budget.

## Job-shop manufacturing: one-off and small-batch fabrication (job-shop-manufacturing)
- **Open:** "What part or product is it, and do you have a drawing or a sample?" then "What material, and how many?"
- **Then ask (in order, one or two at a time):** process (CNC routing/milling, laser cutting, turning, welding, sheet-metal bending, 3D printing, moulding); material and grade (mild steel, stainless 304/316, aluminium, wood/MDF, plastics); dimensions and tolerances; finish (powder coat with RAL colour, galvanising, anodising, paint, polish); quantity (prototype, batch, repeat); assembly and packaging; delivery or installation; certificates or test reports needed; timeline; budget.
- **Photo/drawing tells you:** shape, approximate size. **It cannot tell you:** tolerances, material grade, load. Ask for a dimensioned drawing or CAD file (DWG, DXF, STEP). If there is only a sample, the supplier measures it and reverse-engineers the part.
- **Price drivers (spec decisions):** material and grade; machine time and complexity; tolerances; finishing; quantity (set-up spread over the run); assembly; delivery; certification.
- **Misconceptions to correct:** "One piece costs the same per unit as a hundred" (set-up time dominates small runs). Mild steel rusts quickly in humid, coastal UAE air without galvanising or a proper coating. Near the sea, use 316 stainless, or galvanised steel with a powder coat.
- **Complete quotable request =** part description; drawing/CAD or sample; material and grade; dimensions and tolerances; process; finish; quantity; assembly/packaging; delivery/installation; certificates; location; timeline; budget.
