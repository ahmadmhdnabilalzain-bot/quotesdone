# Conversation playbook: Outdoor and pools

**Categories:** landscaping, swimming-pools

## Landscaping and irrigation (landscaping)
- **Open:** Is this a new garden (bare sand or after construction), a redesign of an existing one, or ongoing maintenance? Villa, townhouse, building podium/roof, or commercial site, and which community and emirate?
- **Then ask (in order, one or two at a time):**
  - Area: rough length x width of each zone (front, back, side strips, roof); a plot plan or sketch with dimensions is best.
  - Softscape: natural grass, artificial turf, planting beds, trees/palms (count and approximate height), shrubs, ground cover, gravel or mulch.
  - Hardscape: paving, pathways, stepping stones, edging, raised planters, pergola/shade structure, seating, BBQ counter, water feature, outdoor lighting.
  - Irrigation: new system or repair; drip for beds, sprinklers/pop-ups for lawn, bubblers for trees; automatic controller wanted; number of zones.
  - Water source: TSE (treated recycled water) or potable mains, and who arranges the connection (DM / DEWA, the emirate's utility, or the community).
  - Site condition: existing soil (sand, rubble, construction debris), levels and drainage, removal of old plants or turf, access width (machinery or wheelbarrow only).
  - Maintenance: one-off install only, or a contract; visit frequency; whether it covers trimming, fertilising, plant pest treatment, irrigation checks, green-waste removal.
  - Timeline and season: summer planting is harder; outdoor work loses midday hours from June to September.
  - Budget range and priorities (low water use, low maintenance, shade, privacy, kids/pets).
- **Photo/drawing tells you:** current state, rough shape, existing trees, paving, sun and shade, access gate. **It cannot tell you:** exact dimensions, soil below the surface, water pressure, buried services (irrigation lines, cables, drainage), level differences. Ask for measured dimensions or a plot plan; say a supplier site visit confirms levels and services before the final price.
- **Price drivers (spec decisions):** total area and number of zones; turf type (natural vs artificial, pile height and density for artificial); plant species and sizes, especially mature trees and palms; soil replacement or improvement depth; irrigation type, controller and zones; hardscape material (natural stone, interlock, porcelain, concrete) and thickness; lighting fixtures and cabling; access (narrow side gate, roof garden needing crane lift or manual carry); green-waste and debris removal; maintenance frequency and contract length; community/landlord NOC; permitted working hours in the community.
- **Misconceptions to correct:**
  - "Grass is easy to keep": natural lawn in UAE heat needs daily irrigation and regular care; artificial turf gets very hot in direct sun.
  - "Any plant will do": heat and salt-tolerant species survive; the supplier recommends, the customer approves.
  - "Irrigation is just pipes": controller, zoning, filtration and the water source matter, and TSE vs potable is set by the utility or community, not the contractor.
  - "No approval needed": many communities require a developer/landlord NOC for hardscape, pergolas or plot changes. In Dubai, landscaping is reported to be a classified contracting activity registered with DM (unverified), so the request should ask the supplier for its registration.
- **Complete quotable request =**
  - emirate, community, property type (villa / townhouse / podium / roof / commercial)
  - zones with dimensions or a plot plan, plus photos
  - scope split: softscape, hardscape, irrigation, lighting, maintenance
  - grass type, plant list or preference, tree/palm count and size
  - hardscape material preference and areas
  - irrigation type, controller, water source (TSE or potable) and who arranges the connection
  - site condition, removal of debris or old plants, access width
  - maintenance frequency and contract length (if any)
  - NOC status, working-hour limits, target start and finish dates, budget range

## Swimming pools (swimming-pools)
- **Open:** New pool construction, renovation of an existing pool, or maintenance/repair? Private villa, building/community pool, or hotel/commercial?
- **Then ask (in order, one or two at a time):**
  - New build: preferred size (length x width), depth profile (constant, shallow-to-deep, plunge), shape (rectangular, freeform, infinity/overflow edge), in-ground or rooftop/terrace.
  - Location on plot: distance from boundary walls and the house; on a roof or terrace a structural engineer must confirm the slab can carry it.
  - Structure: reinforced concrete (cast or sprayed) or a prefabricated/fibreglass shell.
  - Finish: tiles (glass mosaic, porcelain), plaster/render, pebble finish; coping stone; deck around the pool and its material.
  - Filtration: skimmer or overflow; sand, cartridge or glass-media filter; pump sizing is the supplier's job from the volume.
  - Water treatment: chlorine dosing, salt chlorination, UV or similar; automatic dosing control wanted or not.
  - Extras: heating or cooling (a chiller matters in UAE summer), lighting, jets or counter-current swim, water features, steps/benches, pool cover, safety fence or barrier for children.
  - Plant room: where the pump and filter sit, space available, noise concerns.
  - Renovation: what is failing (leak, cracked or loose tiles, cloudy or green water, noisy pump, old lighting); pool size and age.
  - Maintenance: visit frequency, whether chemicals are included, water testing, filter cleaning, equipment checks; contract length.
  - Timeline and budget range.
- **Photo/drawing tells you:** shape, finish, surroundings, visible damage, labels on plant-room equipment. **It cannot tell you:** soil and groundwater conditions, buried services, the load capacity of a roof, the source of a leak, the true water volume. Ask for dimensions and depths. For leaks, say a supplier leak test on site finds the cause. For new builds, the soil test and structural design come from a licensed engineering consultant.
- **Price drivers (spec decisions):** size and depth (volume); structure type; overflow/infinity edge vs skimmer; tile and finish grade; coping and deck material; filtration and treatment system; heating/chiller; lighting and features; excavation, rock or groundwater (dewatering); access for excavators and concrete trucks; rooftop structural works; safety fencing; maintenance frequency and whether chemicals are included; community/landlord NOC; working hours.
- **Misconceptions to correct:**
  - "A pool is a hole with tiles": it is structural, waterproofing, plumbing and electrical work in one.
  - "No permit in my own garden": a new villa pool needs a building permit from the authority for that area (Dubai Municipality, Trakhees, Dubai Development Authority, or the emirate's municipality, depending on location), usually applied for through a licensed engineering consultant, plus the community developer's NOC. The authority decides, not the contractor; the agent records who will apply and proceeds.
  - "Pool water stays cool": UAE pools get very warm in summer; ask whether a chiller is wanted.
  - "Salt pools have no chlorine": a salt system makes chlorine from the salt.
  - "A fence is optional": child-safety barriers are strongly advised and some communities require them.
- **Complete quotable request =**
  - emirate, community, property type, ground level or rooftop
  - new / renovation / maintenance
  - dimensions, depth profile, shape, edge type
  - structure type, finish, coping and deck material
  - filtration and treatment preference, heating/chiller, lighting and extras
  - plant-room location and site access for machinery
  - renovation: problem description, pool size and age, photos
  - maintenance: frequency, chemicals included or not, contract length
  - permit/NOC status and who applies (consultant or contractor), safety barrier needs
  - target start and finish dates, budget range
