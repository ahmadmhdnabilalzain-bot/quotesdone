# Conversation playbook: Electrical, power and smart home

**Categories:** electrical, generators, solar, smart-home

## Electrical works (electrical)
- **Open:** What needs doing: new points, a repair or fault, a DB/load upgrade, lighting, or full rewiring of a fit-out? Which emirate and what type of property (villa, apartment, office, shop, warehouse)?
- **Must-haves (asked in this order, one or two a turn, then the summary):** scope: what is being installed or fixed (the item, its rating and how many); services: the cable route and its distance from the main board, indoors or outdoors; performance: the supply (single or three phase) and the spare load at the board; permits: DEWA or building approval; location; timing
- **Then ask (in order, one or two at a time):**
  - Is power on now and is anything unsafe (tripping, burning smell, hot sockets)? If yes: switch off at the breaker and treat as urgent.
  - Scope count: number of sockets, switches, light points, isolators (AC, water heater, cooker), outdoor points.
  - Existing or new: extending an existing circuit, or new circuits from the DB? Is there spare way in the DB?
  - Load change: adding AC, EV charger, cooker, heaters, a server room? Does the meter/main breaker need upgrading?
  - Concealed in wall/ceiling (chasing, conduit) or surface trunking? Gypsum ceiling access available?
  - Lighting: type (downlights, LED strip, track, chandelier), colour temperature, dimming, emergency lighting needed?
  - Finish of accessories: standard white, metal plate, designer range; colour.
  - Commercial fit-out: approved drawings exist? Who submits to the utility and gets the completion/inspection?
  - Occupied or vacant; allowed working hours; building-management NOC for fit-out.
  - Timeline and whether testing certificate is required at handover.
- **Photo/drawing tells you:** DB make-up and number of ways, existing accessories, ceiling type, visible damage, room layout. **It cannot tell you:** cable sizes, what is behind the wall, earth condition, actual load, DB rating; ask for the DB label and main breaker rating, or say a site inspection by the electrician decides it.
- **Price drivers (spec decisions):** point count; cable size and type (LSZH where specified); conduit concealed vs surface; DB replacement or added ways, RCD/RCBO protection; accessory range and finish; luminaire supply by contractor or by customer; height and scaffold; chasing and making good (plaster/paint) included or not; debris removal; utility drawing submission and inspection (DEWA in Dubai, ADDC/AADC under TAQA Distribution in Abu Dhabi, SEWA in Sharjah, EtihadWE in the Northern Emirates); landlord/building-management NOC; night or weekend work in malls and occupied offices.
- **Misconceptions to correct:**
  - "Just add a socket" on a full circuit is not small; the circuit or DB may need upgrading.
  - Load increase is decided by the utility, not the contractor; it needs a submission.
  - Making good after chasing is often excluded unless asked for. Ask suppliers to state it.
  - Any licensed handyman is not enough for new circuits; the contractor must be registered with the emirate's utility.
- **Complete quotable request =** emirate and area; property type; occupied/vacant; scope list with point counts; new vs existing circuits; load additions; concealed or surface; accessory finish; luminaires supplied by whom; making good included y/n; drawings/photos of DB; NOC/working-hours limits; testing certificate y/n; target date.

## Generators (generators)
- **Open:** What is the generator for: standby when grid fails, prime power on a site with no grid, or an event/temporary hire? Buy or rent?
- **Must-haves (asked in this order, one or two a turn, then the summary):** usage: what it is for (standby, prime power or an event) and buy or rent; performance: the load it must carry (kVA or the equipment on it); system: fuel, silent canopy and changeover; location; timing
- **Then ask (in order, one or two at a time):**
  - What must it run: whole building or essential loads only (lifts, pumps, lighting, servers, cold rooms)? Any motor starting loads?
  - Known size in kVA, or the connected load list / main breaker rating so suppliers can size it.
  - Prime or standby rating; expected running hours per day.
  - Changeover: automatic (ATS/AMF panel) or manual; who integrates it with the existing DB?
  - Fuel: diesel tank size and autonomy wanted; day tank or bulk tank; refuelling service needed?
  - Enclosure: open set indoors in a plant room, or canopy/soundproof outdoor; noise limits near residents?
  - Location and access: ground, roof, basement; crane or forklift access; plinth needed?
  - Rental: duration, delivery and pick-up, cables and distribution boards, operator, fuel included or not.
  - Maintenance contract after supply?
- **Photo/drawing tells you:** available space, access route, existing plant room, DB location. **It cannot tell you:** actual load or starting current; ask for the load list or the main breaker rating, or a load study by the supplier's engineer.
- **Price drivers (spec decisions):** kVA rating and prime/standby; engine and alternator origin; canopy and sound level; ATS/AMF panel and synchronising for multiple sets; cable run length and size; fuel tank capacity; exhaust and ventilation routing; crane lifting; plinth/civil works; commissioning and load bank test; rental period, operator, fuel; utility drawing approval for the changeover so the set never back-feeds the grid; civil defence and municipality conditions for fuel storage; landlord/master developer NOC.
- **Misconceptions to correct:**
  - kVA is not kW: sets are rated at a power factor, so a set "the same number" as the load in kW is undersized.
  - Starting current of motors, ACs and lifts needs margin; size on the load list, not on a guess.
  - A changeover that is not interlocked is dangerous and will not be approved.
  - Rental quotes vary mainly by what is included (fuel, operator, cables); compare like for like.
- **Complete quotable request =** buy/rent; standby/prime; kVA or load list; essential loads; changeover type; fuel autonomy; indoor/outdoor and noise limit; location and access; rental duration (if rental); maintenance y/n; emirate; NOC status; date needed.

## Solar PV (solar)
- **Open:** Grid-connected to cut the electricity bill, off-grid (farm, remote site), or hybrid with batteries for backup? Which emirate?
- **Must-haves (asked in this order, one or two a turn, then the summary):** performance: the electricity use (summer bills); size: usable roof area, orientation and shade; system: grid-connected or with batteries; ownership; location; timing
- **Then ask (in order, one or two at a time):**
  - Property type and ownership; owner, or tenant with landlord consent?
  - Usable roof area, roof type (concrete flat, metal sheet, carport, ground mount) and shading (tanks, AC units, neighbouring towers).
  - Monthly consumption: ask for recent utility bills (kWh), not the money amount.
  - Target system size in kWp if known, or goal (offset day load, maximise roof).
  - Batteries wanted? Hours of backup and which loads.
  - Who handles the utility application: Shams Dubai via DEWA, the Abu Dhabi netting scheme via TAQA Distribution, EtihadWE distributed solar in the Northern Emirates; Sharjah to be confirmed with SEWA.
  - Monitoring app, cleaning and maintenance contract wanted?
  - Structure: ballasted or anchored; any roof waterproofing work due first?
- **Photo/drawing tells you:** roof layout, obstructions, rough area, access. **It cannot tell you:** structural capacity, exact shading over the year, roof waterproofing condition; a site survey by the installer and, where needed, a structural check decide it.
- **Price drivers (spec decisions):** kWp size; panel type and efficiency class; inverter type (string, hybrid, micro) and kW; battery chemistry and kWh; mounting structure (ballast, anchor, elevated carport); cable routes and DB integration; meter change by the utility; roof access and lifting; waterproofing protection; monitoring; cleaning contract; approvals: DEWA solar NOC and design approval in Dubai, distribution company approval in Abu Dhabi, EtihadWE connection conditions; master developer/community and building-management NOC.
- **Misconceptions to correct:**
  - In Dubai only DEWA-enrolled solar contractors can apply; ask suppliers to confirm their enrolment.
  - Net metering credits export against consumption; it is not a cash sale of power, and scheme scope can change, so the utility confirms eligibility.
  - Grid-tied without batteries shuts down in a power cut for safety; backup needs a hybrid system.
  - Dust lowers output; cleaning is part of the plan.
- **Complete quotable request =** emirate; property type and ownership/consent; roof type and usable area; shading notes; consumption in kWh from bills; grid-tied/hybrid/off-grid; battery need; target kWp or goal; monitoring and cleaning y/n; NOC status; photos of roof and DB; timeline.

## Smart home (smart-home)
- **Open:** What should be smart: lighting, curtains/blinds, AC, security, intercom, audio, or all? New build/renovation, or an existing finished home?
- **Must-haves (asked in this order, one or two a turn, then the summary):** scope: what should be smart (lighting, curtains, AC, security); size: rooms and floors; system: wired or wireless, one app; existing: a new build or a finished home (no breaking walls); location; timing
- **Then ask (in order, one or two at a time):**
  - Property size: number of rooms and floors; villa or apartment.
  - Wired system (needs cabling, best at fit-out) or wireless retrofit (no wall chasing)?
  - Control: wall keypads, app, voice assistant, scenes ("good night", "away").
  - Lighting: number of circuits, dimming, which rooms.
  - Curtains/blinds: how many motors, track lengths, sheer plus blackout.
  - AC: type (split, ducted, VRF) and whether the AC system allows integration.
  - Security tie-in: cameras, door lock, video intercom, alarm.
  - Network: is Wi-Fi coverage good everywhere? Is a rack location available?
  - Programming, training and support after handover.
- **Photo/drawing tells you:** room layout, existing switch positions, curtain widths, ceiling type. **It cannot tell you:** if neutral wires are in switch boxes, back-box depth, network quality, AC compatibility; ask for a photo of an open switch box, or the integrator's site survey decides it.
- **Price drivers (spec decisions):** wired vs wireless protocol; number of devices, keypads, motors; keypad finish; central controller and rack; network upgrade (access points, switch); cabling and chasing; curtain track and fabric supply; AC gateways; integration with intercom/CCTV; programming hours; warranty and support; TDRA-approved wireless devices and ECAS-certified electrical products; building-management NOC; working hours in occupied buildings.
- **Misconceptions to correct:**
  - Smart switches often need a neutral wire; many older boxes do not have one.
  - "Everything in one app" depends on the systems chosen; integration is a design decision, not automatic.
  - A weak network makes any smart home unreliable; network is part of the scope.
  - Retrofitting wired systems after finishes means chasing and repainting.
- **Complete quotable request =** emirate; property type, rooms and floors; new or retrofit; wired/wireless preference; systems list with counts (light circuits, motors and track lengths, AC units, locks, cameras); control methods; finish of keypads; network condition; integration needs; support expectation; NOC/working hours; timeline.
