# Conversation playbook: Services and maintenance

**Categories:** cleaning, facade-cleaning, pest, movers, general-services-other

## Cleaning (cleaning)
- **Open:** What kind of cleaning: regular (daily/weekly), one-off deep clean, move-in/move-out, post-construction, or a specialist job (kitchen exhaust, sofa/carpet, tanks)? Home, office, retail, or building common areas?
- **Then ask (in order, one or two at a time):**
  - Size: bedrooms/bathrooms for homes; floor area or number of floors for offices and buildings.
  - Furnished or empty; any areas excluded.
  - Surfaces and items: floors (marble, tile, wood, carpet), windows inside only or outside reachable from balconies, kitchen appliances inside, upholstery, mattresses, curtains.
  - Post-construction: paint splashes, cement or grout haze, adhesive, dust in AC grilles.
  - Frequency and shift: daily, weekly, monthly; hours per visit; number of cleaners; day or night shift for offices.
  - Materials: supplier brings equipment and consumables, or the client supplies; any eco or fragrance-free preference.
  - Access: building permit/gate pass, parking, lift booking, working-hour rules.
  - Contract length for regular service; start date; budget range.
- **Photo/drawing tells you:** level of dirt, surface types, clutter. **It cannot tell you:** total area, hidden stains, grease build-up inside exhausts, what is behind furniture. Ask for area or room count; say the supplier may ask to see the site for post-construction or exhaust jobs.
- **Price drivers (spec decisions):** area and room count; deep vs routine; post-construction residue; specialist items (upholstery, carpet shampoo, kitchen exhaust ducts); frequency, hours and headcount; contract length; materials and equipment included; night or weekend shifts; access, parking and building rules.
- **Misconceptions to correct:**
  - "Deep clean = regular clean, just longer": it covers inside appliances, grout, fixtures and high areas; list them.
  - "Window cleaning includes the outside of a tower": outside glass above reachable height is facade cleaning (rope access/BMU), a separate service.
  - "Any cleaning company can do anything": the supplier's licence activity must cover the exact service (general cleaning and building/exterior cleaning are separate activities).
- **Complete quotable request =** emirate and area; property type; size (rooms or floor area); furnished or empty; service type (regular/deep/move/post-construction/specialist); items and surfaces in scope and exclusions; frequency, hours, headcount, shift; materials supplied by whom; access and parking rules; contract length; start date; budget range.

## Facade cleaning: rope access and BMU (facade-cleaning)
- **Open:** Which building (type, number of floors or height) and which facade surfaces: glass only, or cladding, stone, signage too? Is there a BMU (roof cradle) on the building?
- **Then ask (in order, one or two at a time):**
  - Access method: BMU, rope access, mast climber, boom lift for low-rise; if unsure, say the supplier proposes it after a survey.
  - Who provides anchors or the BMU, and whether BMU inspection/certification records are current (held by the building owner or facility manager).
  - Area or number of elevations; any atrium, skylights, canopies, louvres.
  - Inside glass too, or outside only.
  - Frequency: one-off, or a contract (for example quarterly); contract length.
  - Water and power on the roof; where run-off goes; protecting balconies, cars, pedestrians below.
  - Working hours allowed by the building/community; wind limits apply on the day.
- **Photo/drawing tells you:** facade type, approximate floors, visible stains. **It cannot tell you:** anchor locations, BMU condition, total glass area, stain type (hard-water, salt, cement). Ask for elevation drawings or floor count and width; the supplier's site survey confirms the method.
- **Price drivers (spec decisions):** height and total area; access method (BMU vs rope access vs lift); anchor installation or certification if missing; surface type and stain removal (hard-water or cement residue needs special treatment); inside + outside; frequency and contract length; working hours (night or early morning), ground barricading; building NOC.
- **Misconceptions to correct:**
  - "Any cleaner can go on ropes": in Dubai, DM guideline DM-HSD-GU35 says rope access only by specialist, certified companies and personnel (secondary sources cite IRATA, unverified). Ask the supplier for its certification.
  - "The BMU is the cleaner's problem": the BMU belongs to the building; its inspection records come from the owner or facility manager.
  - "It can be done any day": wind and summer heat stop work; schedules need margin.
- **Complete quotable request =** emirate, building type, floors/height; facade surfaces and elevations (drawings or photos); access method or request for a survey; BMU/anchor availability and records; inside/outside; stain types; frequency and contract length; water/power on roof; allowed working hours; building NOC holder; start date; budget range.

## Pest control (pest)
- **Open:** Which pest (cockroaches, bed bugs, ants, termites, rodents, mosquitoes/flies, birds, snakes or scorpions), and is it active now or preventive?
- **Then ask (in order, one or two at a time):**
  - Property: apartment, villa, office, restaurant kitchen, warehouse; size (rooms or area); indoor, garden, or both.
  - Where seen and how bad: one room, whole unit, neighbours affected too.
  - Treatment type: general spray/gel, bed-bug heat or chemical, termite (pre- or post-construction), fumigation, bird netting/spikes, rodent baiting and proofing.
  - Occupants: children, pets, pregnancy, allergies; vacate needed or not.
  - One-off with follow-up visits, or an annual/periodic contract; written warranty wanted.
  - Commercial food premises: does it need a service report for inspections.
  - Access times, building approvals, start date, budget range.
- **Photo/drawing tells you:** pest identification, droppings, damage. **It cannot tell you:** the extent of infestation, nests inside walls, entry points. Ask where and when they are seen; the supplier inspects before treating termites, rodents or bed bugs.
- **Price drivers (spec decisions):** pest type; area and number of rooms; treatment method; DM-approved/federally registered products (MoCCAE registration); number of follow-up visits; warranty period; contract frequency and length; vacate time; proofing works (sealing gaps, netting); commercial reporting; working hours.
- **Misconceptions to correct:**
  - "One spray solves bed bugs": it needs repeat treatment and the customer's preparation (laundry, decluttering).
  - "Any company can fumigate": in Dubai, the DM authorised list covers general pest control only; fumigation, termite and bird control are excluded from it, so these need specific approval. The supplier must be on the current DM list (Dubai) and use registered pesticides.
  - "DIY products are the same": professional products are regulated and must be applied by qualified staff.
- **Complete quotable request =** emirate and area; property type and size; pest type and severity; locations affected; indoor/outdoor; treatment method if known; occupants and vacate tolerance; one-off or contract, follow-ups and warranty; reporting needs; access times; start date; budget range.

## Movers (movers)
- **Open:** Moving from where to where (emirate/area to emirate/area, or international), and what size: studio, bedroom count, villa, or office?
- **Then ask (in order, one or two at a time):**
  - Inventory: large items (beds, wardrobes, sofas, fridge, washing machine), boxes estimate, fragile items (glass, art, piano), heavy items (safe, gym equipment).
  - Services: packing, unpacking, dismantle/reassemble furniture, curtain/TV/AC unit removal and fixing, appliance disconnection.
  - Access at both ends: floor, lift size and booking, loading bay, parking, building move-out/move-in permit, walking distance to truck.
  - Storage needed between dates, and for how long.
  - Packing materials supplied by mover; wardrobe boxes; crating for art.
  - Insurance cover for goods in transit wanted.
  - Moving date, flexibility, time windows imposed by buildings; weekend or night moves.
  - Budget range.
- **Photo/drawing tells you:** volume and item types (a room-by-room video is best). **It cannot tell you:** lift size, stairs, building time windows, what is in closed cupboards. Ask for both buildings' rules; a survey (in person or video) firms up the truck size.
- **Price drivers (spec decisions):** volume (truck count/size) and crew size; distance and inter-emirate travel; packing/unpacking; dismantling and installation; fragile and heavy items; floors, lift availability and carrying distance; storage; insurance; date, weekend or night slots; building permits.
- **Misconceptions to correct:**
  - "The building will just let us in": most towers need a move permit and lift booking from building management; arrange it early.
  - "Mover connects everything": AC, gas and plumbing reconnection may need other trades.
  - "Insurance is automatic": cover and its limits must be stated in the quote.
- **Complete quotable request =** origin and destination (emirate, area, floor); property size; inventory list or video; fragile/heavy items; services (packing, dismantling, installation, unpacking); access and permits at both ends; storage; insurance; date and time window; budget range.

## Other services (general-services-other)
Use this method for any trade not covered by another playbook. Never map it to the nearest trade; record the exact trade.
- **Open:** What exactly needs doing, in one sentence, and what is the result wanted? Is it new work, repair, replacement or a recurring service?
- **Then ask (in order, one or two at a time):**
  - Identify the trade: name it precisely (for example signage, glazing film, upholstery, sign installation, welding repair). If it involves a regulated trade (pest, rope access, electrical, gas), apply that trade's rules.
  - Scope: list each item or task; what is included and what is excluded; who removes old items.
  - Quantities: counts, dimensions (length x width x height), areas, weights, number of visits.
  - Materials and standard: material, grade, finish, colour, any specification, drawings, sample or reference photo; any standard or authority approval the job must meet.
  - Site: emirate, area, property type, indoor/outdoor, floor level.
  - Access: lift, parking, height (ladder, scaffold, lift), working hours, building/community NOC, permits.
  - Timeline: start date, deadline, phasing, shutdown constraints.
  - Recurring work: frequency and contract length.
  - Budget range.
- **Photo/drawing tells you:** what the item looks like and its condition. **It cannot tell you:** dimensions, hidden condition, material grade, site access. Ask for measurements and a reference; say a site visit by the supplier confirms details where needed.
- **Price drivers (spec decisions):** quantity and size; material and finish grade; complexity or custom fabrication; height and access; working hours; frequency and contract length; permits/NOCs; removal and disposal.
- **Misconceptions to correct:**
  - "The supplier will figure out the details": missing specs mean the quote is held; exact specs give comparable quotes.
  - "Any licensed company can do it": the supplier's licence activity must cover the exact service.
- **Complete quotable request =** precise trade name; scope list with inclusions/exclusions; quantities and dimensions; materials, grade, finish and any standard; reference photos/drawings; emirate, area, property type; access, height, working hours, NOC/permits; timeline; frequency and contract length if recurring; budget range.
