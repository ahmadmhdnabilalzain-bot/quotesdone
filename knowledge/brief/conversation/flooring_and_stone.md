# Conversation playbook: Flooring and stone

**Categories:** flooring, tiling, marble-granite, epoxy-flooring

General rules for this group:
- Area alone never prices a floor. Always pin down: what is there now, what goes down, where (room type, indoor/outdoor, wet/dry), and who handles the old finish.
- Ask one or two questions at a time; confirm what the customer already said instead of asking again.
- Apartment and community jobs: the building management or master developer usually issues the NOC and sets working hours, noise limits and debris rules. Name them; do not guess their rules.
- Fit-out permit (DM, DDA, Trakhees or the relevant municipality) is normally needed for commercial floor works and for anything that touches screed levels, waterproofing or structure. Building management confirms whether a residential retile needs one.

## Flooring (flooring)
Covers vinyl/LVT/SPC, laminate, engineered and solid wood, raised access floors, rubber, and general floor replacement. Tile, stone and epoxy have their own sections.
- **Open:** Which space and roughly how big (m2 or room dimensions)? What finish do you want, and what is on the floor now?
- **Must-haves (asked in this order, one or two a turn, then the summary):** size: the area and which rooms; usage: how the floor is used (traffic, heavy impact zones, wet areas); existing: what is on the floor now and its condition; finish: the flooring type, thickness and colour; occupancy: occupied or empty during the work; location; timing
- **Then ask (in order, one or two at a time):**
  - Property type (villa, apartment, office, shop, warehouse) and floor level; lift access for materials?
  - Existing floor: tile, marble, screed, old carpet, old wood? Keep and lay over, or remove?
  - Product type: SPC/LVT click, glue-down vinyl, laminate, engineered wood, solid wood, sports or rubber floor.
  - Thickness/wear layer and use class (home, light commercial, heavy commercial).
  - Underlay type (acoustic underlay is often a building rule in apartments); moisture barrier on ground floors.
  - Skirting: new, reuse, material and height; profiles at doorways and transitions to tile.
  - Door undercutting needed after the floor height rises?
  - Furniture moving: empty site or occupied?
  - Timeline and working-hour limits; budget band or quality tier (entry, mid, premium) without naming figures.
- **Photo/drawing tells you:** current finish, approximate room shape, skirting style, visible damage, door positions. **It cannot tell you:** exact area, floor flatness, hidden moisture, what is under the existing finish, ceiling/door clearance. Ask for a dimensioned plan or tape measurements; flatness and moisture need the supplier's site check.
- **Price drivers (spec decisions):** product type and grade, wear layer, plank size and pattern (herringbone and chevron take more labour and waste), underlay, levelling compound if the subfloor is out of tolerance, removal and disposal of old floor, skirting and trims, door trimming, access floor/lift booking, night or weekend working, building NOC and fit-out permit for commercial.
- **Misconceptions to correct:**
  - "Waterproof" SPC/LVT survives spills, not standing water or a leaking subfloor; joints and edges still need care.
  - Solid wood moves with humidity; in UAE AC-on/AC-off homes engineered wood is usually the safer choice.
  - Engineered wood and laminate are not for bathrooms or other wet areas: standing water and steam swell and lift them. To keep the look continuous, use a wood-effect porcelain tile in the bathrooms (waterproofed beneath), with a transition profile at the door. Say this before asking anything else. (Ledger 1200 scenario bank, 27 Oct 2026.)
  - Laying over old tile is possible only if the tile is sound and flat; hollow tiles must be fixed first.
  - Floor height rise affects doors, wardrobes, kitchen plinths and thresholds.
- **Complete quotable request =** location/emirate and property type; area per room (m2) or dimensions; existing finish and removal yes/no; product type, thickness/wear layer, colour/size; pattern; underlay; skirting and trims; door trimming; furniture moving; occupied or empty; working-hour limits and NOC status; target start date.

## Tiling (tiling)
- **Open:** Floors, walls, or both, and in which rooms? Are you replacing old tiles or tiling a new/bare surface?
- **Must-haves (asked in this order, one or two a turn, then the summary):** size: the area and which rooms (floors, walls or both); existing: old tiles to remove or tile over; finish: tile size, type and finish; occupancy: occupied or empty during the work; location; timing
- **Then ask (in order, one or two at a time):**
  - Area per room (m2) or room dimensions and wall height to be tiled (full height or half).
  - Wet area? Bathrooms, kitchens, balconies, pool decks need waterproofing; is it included or already done?
  - Tile type: porcelain (glazed or full-body), ceramic, mosaic, large-format slab. Customer supplies tiles or supplier supplies?
  - Tile size (60x60, 60x120, 120x120, large slabs) and finish (matt, polished, anti-slip/R-rating for wet and outdoor).
  - Pattern: straight, brick bond, herringbone, diagonal; feature walls or niches.
  - Grout: cement or epoxy grout; joint width; colour.
  - Removal of old tiles and screed; screed levelling; falls to drains in showers.
  - Edges and trims: aluminium/stainless profiles, mitred edges, stair nosing.
  - Outdoor/balcony: sun-exposed? Movement joints and heat-rated adhesive matter.
  - Occupied home? Timeline and working hours.
- **Photo/drawing tells you:** current tile size and layout, room shape, visible cracks or hollow tiles, existing drains and fixtures. **It cannot tell you:** waterproofing condition, screed thickness, hollowness, true wall plumb. Ask for dimensions; condition of what is under the tile is confirmed on site.
- **Price drivers (spec decisions):** tile type, size and grade (large format needs more skill, levelling clips, sometimes two people per tile); who supplies tiles; pattern complexity and cuts; adhesive class; epoxy vs cement grout; waterproofing membrane; demolition, debris removal and disposal; screed and falls; trims and mitres; sanitary-ware removal and refit; access/height for walls; building NOC; night work.
- **Misconceptions to correct:**
  - Grout is not waterproofing; a wet area needs a membrane under the tile.
  - Polished tiles in wet or outdoor areas are slippery; ask for an anti-slip rating.
  - Retiling a bathroom usually means taking out and refitting WC, vanity and shower glass; include it.
  - Order extra tiles for cuts and future repairs from the same batch.
  - Large slabs need a flat substrate; uneven walls add levelling work.
- **Complete quotable request =** emirate and property type; rooms and areas (floor m2, wall m2 and height); remove old yes/no; tile type, size, finish, who supplies; pattern; grout type; waterproofing scope; trims; sanitary removal/refit; outdoor exposure; occupied or empty; NOC/working hours; start date.

## Marble and granite (marble-granite)
Covers marble, granite, quartz/engineered stone, travertine and other natural stone for floors, walls, stairs, countertops, vanity tops, cladding, and polishing/restoration.
- **Open:** Is this new stone installation, or polishing/restoration of existing stone? Where: floor, stairs, kitchen top, vanity, wall or outdoor?
- **Must-haves (asked in this order, one or two a turn, then the summary):** scope: new stone or polishing existing stone, and where; size; finish: stone type and finish; location; timing
- **Then ask (in order, one or two at a time):**
  - For new stone: material (marble, granite, quartz, travertine, porcelain slab as alternative) and colour/name if known; customer supplies or supplier supplies?
  - Dimensions: floor m2; countertop length x depth and number of pieces; stairs count, tread and riser sizes, landings.
  - Thickness (common 18-20 mm for floors, 20-30 mm for tops) and edge profile (eased, bevel, bullnose, mitred apron).
  - Finish: polished, honed, leathered, flamed (outdoor/anti-slip).
  - Cut-outs: sink (undermount or top-mount), hob, taps; backsplash height.
  - Book-matching or vein direction required?
  - Fixing: tile adhesive, mortar bed, mechanical anchors for wall cladding.
  - For restoration: area, problems (dull, etched, scratched, stained, lippage, cracked grout), and whether crystallisation, grinding, honing or sealing is wanted.
  - Occupied home? Dust and slurry control; furniture moving; timeline.
- **Photo/drawing tells you:** stone type roughly, condition (dullness, stains, cracks), layout, edge styles. **It cannot tell you:** true stone identity, etch depth, lippage height, whether stains are inside the stone. Restoration scope and template sizes are confirmed by the supplier's site visit; templates for countertops are taken after base cabinets are fixed.
- **Price drivers (spec decisions):** stone type and grade/origin, slab size and wastage for matching, thickness, edge profile, number of cut-outs, finish, book-matching, templating and site measure, transport and lifting (stairs, high floors, crane for heavy slabs), fixing method, sealing, removal of old stone, polishing method and passes, night work, building NOC.
- **Misconceptions to correct:**
  - Marble etches with lemon, vinegar and many cleaners; sealing slows staining but does not stop etching. For kitchens, granite or quartz is more forgiving.
  - Quartz is not heat-proof; hot pots can mark it. It also fades in direct outdoor sun.
  - Polishing does not fix cracked or hollow stone; that needs repair or replacement first.
  - Natural stone varies slab to slab; a showroom sample is indicative only.
- **Complete quotable request =** emirate and property type; new or restoration; material and colour; who supplies; dimensions/areas and piece counts; thickness; finish; edge profile; cut-outs and backsplash; matching; fixing method; removal yes/no; floor level and lift access; restoration problems list; occupied or empty; NOC/working hours; start date.

## Epoxy flooring (epoxy-flooring)
Covers epoxy, PU and PU-cement resin floors, self-levelling, roller coats, anti-slip, ESD, decorative metallic/flake, car parks and garages.
- **Open:** What is the space used for (garage, warehouse, factory, kitchen, car park, showroom, home)? Roughly how many m2?
- **Must-haves (asked in this order, one or two a turn, then the summary):** usage: what the space is used for; size; existing: the floor's condition; location; timing
- **Then ask (in order, one or two at a time):**
  - Substrate: new concrete (how old), old concrete, existing tiles, old coating to remove?
  - Traffic: foot only, cars, forklifts, pallet trucks, chemicals, hot water, steam cleaning?
  - System: roller coat, self-levelling, mortar screed; PU-cement for commercial kitchens, food plants and thermal shock.
  - Thickness requirement or performance spec from a consultant?
  - Finish: gloss/matt, colour, anti-slip aggregate, decorative flake or metallic, line marking and bay numbers.
  - Indoor or outdoor/sun exposed? (UV yellowing of epoxy; PU topcoat for UV).
  - Coving at wall junctions; drains and falls; expansion joints.
  - Can the area be closed for curing? Weekend or night work?
  - Any client or authority requirement: food-safety, ESD, DCL/low-VOC certificate.
- **Photo/drawing tells you:** current surface, visible cracks, oil stains, old coating peeling, layout. **It cannot tell you:** concrete moisture, strength, contamination depth, flatness. Moisture and adhesion tests are done on site; the quote should state the test and preparation method (grinding or shot-blasting).
- **Price drivers (spec decisions):** system type and thickness, preparation (grinding, shot-blasting, crack and joint repair, old coating removal), moisture-tolerant primer if needed, anti-slip and decorative layers, PU topcoat, coving, line marking, area size and shape, downtime and curing time, night or weekend working, ventilation and odour control in occupied buildings, VOC/DCL requirements on green-rated jobs.
- **Misconceptions to correct:**
  - Epoxy on damp or new concrete fails; new slabs need curing time and a moisture test first.
  - Paint-type epoxy is not the same as a self-levelling or screed system; specify thickness, not just "epoxy".
  - Standard epoxy yellows in UV and can chalk in sun; outdoor areas need a UV-stable topcoat or a different system.
  - Glossy epoxy gets slippery when wet; add anti-slip in wet zones.
  - Hot water and steam cleaning in kitchens need PU-cement, not standard epoxy.
- **Complete quotable request =** emirate and site type; area m2 and layout; substrate type and age; existing coating; traffic and chemical exposure; system and thickness (or performance spec); finish, colour, anti-slip; coving, drains and joints; line marking; indoor/outdoor; downtime allowed; compliance certificates needed; NOC/working hours; start date.
