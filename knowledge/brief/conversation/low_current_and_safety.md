# Conversation playbook: Low current, security and safety

**Categories:** security, it-networking, av-systems, safety

## Security systems: CCTV, access control, intrusion (security)
- **Open:** What do you need to protect and why: home, shop, office, warehouse, building? New system, extension, or replacing an old one? Which emirate?
- **Then ask, CCTV (in order, one or two at a time):**
  - Coverage: which areas must be seen (entrances, gate, parking, cash counter, stock, corridors, lift lobbies, perimeter)? Count the cameras, or the number of doors/zones.
  - Indoor vs outdoor per camera; outdoor needs weatherproof, heat- and dust-rated housings and sun glare consideration.
  - Purpose per view: general overview, or identify faces / read number plates? Identification needs higher resolution, narrower lens or more cameras.
  - Resolution: standard HD, 4MP, 4K/8MP; fixed lens, varifocal, or PTZ for large yards.
  - Night: dark areas need infrared range in metres, or colour-at-night cameras with white light.
  - Recording: NVR (IP cameras) or DVR (analogue/HD-over-coax); number of channels plus spare for growth.
  - Storage days: how many days of recording? Continuous or motion-triggered? Storage size depends on cameras, resolution, frame rate and days.
  - Remote viewing: phone app, multiple users, a monitor on site (size, where)?
  - Audio on cameras? Note privacy limits on recording sound; the supplier confirms what is allowed.
  - Cabling route: new cables, through ceiling or trunking, distance from cameras to recorder, outdoor runs in conduit; PoE switch or separate power; UPS for recorder.
  - Existing cabling reuse: coax or network cable already in place?
- **Then ask, access and intrusion:**
  - Access control: number of doors; reader type (card, PIN, fingerprint, face); door type (glass, wood, metal) for maglock or strike; exit button and emergency release tied to fire alarm; attendance reports needed?
  - Video intercom: villa gate or apartment block, number of indoor monitors.
  - Intrusion alarm: zones, door contacts, motion sensors, panic buttons, siren, monitored by a control centre or self-monitored.
  - Gate/barrier automation if asked: gate type and size, vehicle barrier, ANPR.
- **SIRA (Dubai commercial premises):** if the premises fall under Dubai's security law (Law 12 of 2016, Resolution 1 of 2018), the system must meet SIRA's Preventive Systems Manual (Administrative Resolution 13 of 2025): installer and technicians SIRA-licensed, recordings kept at least 31 days at high quality and at least 10 frames per second, recorder in a secure monitored location with access log, SIRA-approved CCTV warning signs at main entrances and in lifts, wireless components need prior SIRA approval, any change notified to SIRA, annual maintenance contract with a SIRA-registered company. SIRA decides whether a premises is covered and approves any deviation; ask the customer for any SIRA letter or inspection notice. Outside Dubai the police/security authority of the emirate decides; ask the supplier to confirm what applies.
- **Photo/drawing tells you:** layout, entrances, ceiling type, mounting surfaces, existing cameras and recorder, distances roughly. **It cannot tell you:** cable paths behind ceilings, lighting at night, exact distances, network capacity, whether the business is SIRA-regulated; ask for a floor plan with doors marked, a night photo, or say the installer's site survey decides it.
- **Price drivers (spec decisions):** camera count; resolution and lens type; IR/colour night range; indoor vs outdoor housings; PTZ; recorder channels and storage days (drives disk size); RAID or redundancy; monitor; cable length and route (conduit, trunking, ceiling access); PoE switch; UPS; mounting height and lift/scaffold; access door count, reader type, lock type per door; fire-alarm interface; intrusion zones and monitoring; software licences; maintenance contract (mandatory under SIRA where covered); SIRA approval and signage; TDRA approval of wireless devices; building-management or landlord NOC; working hours in malls/occupied offices.
- **Misconceptions to correct:**
  - More megapixels do not fix bad placement; identification depends on distance, lens and light.
  - "30 days storage" is not a free choice for SIRA-covered Dubai premises: minimum is 31 days at the required quality; it sizes the disks.
  - Wi-Fi cameras are not reliable for business sites and wireless parts may need SIRA approval; wired PoE is the norm.
  - A home system bought online cannot be signed off for a regulated commercial premises; the installer must be SIRA-licensed in Dubai.
  - Maglocks must release on fire alarm and power failure; this is life safety, not optional.
- **Complete quotable request =** emirate; premises type and activity; SIRA-covered y/n/unknown; new/extend/replace; CCTV: camera count, indoor/outdoor split, purpose per area, resolution, night requirement, recording days, continuous/motion, remote viewing users, monitor; recorder location; cabling route and distances or floor plan; existing cabling; access: doors, reader type, door types, fire interface; intrusion: zones, monitoring; intercom units; maintenance contract y/n; NOC/working hours; photos and plan; timeline.

## IT networking and structured cabling (it-networking)
- **Open:** Is this new office cabling, Wi-Fi coverage improvement, a server/rack room, or a fault? How many users and workstations?
- **Then ask (in order, one or two at a time):**
  - Data points: count per desk, plus printers, phones, cameras, access points, TVs.
  - Cable category (Cat6, Cat6A) and whether fibre is needed between floors or buildings.
  - Rack: location, size (U), patch panels, cable management, cooling and UPS.
  - Active equipment: switches (PoE needed for phones, cameras, APs), firewall/router, access points count and coverage areas; supply by contractor or customer?
  - Internet: provider (e& or du), line already installed? Telecom room/building riser access?
  - Testing and certification of every link, labelling, as-built drawings.
  - Wi-Fi: area in square metres, wall types (concrete, glass), outdoor coverage, guest network.
  - Ongoing IT support or managed service wanted?
- **Photo/drawing tells you:** office layout, ceiling type, rack location, existing cabling mess. **It cannot tell you:** cable routes through slab and risers, Wi-Fi interference, link quality; ask for a floor plan with desk positions, or a site survey/Wi-Fi survey decides it.
- **Price drivers (spec decisions):** point count; cable category; fibre backbone; containment (cable tray, trunking, floor boxes); rack size and accessories; switch ports and PoE budget; access point count and grade; firewall licensing; testing/certification; labelling and as-builts; ceiling access; TDRA-approved wireless equipment; building-management NOC and riser access; after-hours work.
- **Misconceptions to correct:**
  - More access points is not always better; placement and channel planning matter.
  - Cheap cable without certified testing gives intermittent faults later.
  - The ISP line to the premises is arranged with e& or du, not by the cabling contractor.
- **Complete quotable request =** emirate; premises size and layout; user count; data point schedule; cable category and fibre need; rack location and size; active equipment scope and who supplies; Wi-Fi coverage areas; ISP status; testing and documentation required; support y/n; NOC/working hours; timeline.

## Audio-visual systems (av-systems)
- **Open:** Which space and use: meeting room, boardroom, home cinema, retail/restaurant music, event hall, digital signage? How many rooms?
- **Then ask (in order, one or two at a time):**
  - Room size and seating; ceiling height and type.
  - Display: TV or projector and screen; size; video wall; outdoor display?
  - Conferencing: video calls platform used, camera, microphones (ceiling, table), wireless presentation.
  - Audio: ceiling speakers, zones, background music, paging; home cinema channel layout.
  - Control: simple remote, wall panel, touch panel, room booking panel.
  - Mounting: wall structure (block, gypsum, glass), motorised lift or bracket; cable concealment.
  - Acoustic treatment needed?
  - Integration with lighting/blinds, network and IT policies.
- **Photo/drawing tells you:** room shape, wall types, windows/glare, existing equipment. **It cannot tell you:** acoustics, wall strength behind gypsum, cable paths, network restrictions; ask for room dimensions and wall type, or the integrator's site visit decides it.
- **Price drivers (spec decisions):** display size and type; projector brightness; video wall size; camera and microphone type; speaker count and zones; amplifiers and DSP; control system and programming; cabling and concealment; mounting and backing reinforcement; acoustic panels; network upgrades; TDRA-approved wireless mics and presentation devices; building-management NOC; working hours.
- **Misconceptions to correct:**
  - A bigger TV does not fix a bad meeting room; microphones and acoustics decide call quality.
  - Gypsum walls need reinforcement for heavy displays.
  - Control programming is a real scope item; say who owns later changes.
- **Complete quotable request =** emirate; space type and count; room dimensions and seating; display requirement; conferencing need and platform; audio zones; control method; wall types and mounting; acoustic treatment y/n; integration list; NOC/working hours; photos; timeline.

## Fire and life safety (safety)
- **Open:** What is it: new installation for a fit-out, an extension/modification, a Civil Defence inspection observation, annual maintenance, or extinguisher refill? Which emirate?
- **Then ask (in order, one or two at a time):**
  - Premises type, area, floors, and occupancy (office, restaurant, warehouse, villa, labour accommodation).
  - Systems involved: fire alarm (addressable or conventional, detector count), sprinklers, hose reels, FM-200/other suppression for kitchens or server rooms, kitchen hood suppression, extinguishers, emergency and exit lighting, fire doors.
  - Approved drawings exist? Who submits to Civil Defence (DCD in Dubai, ADCDA in Abu Dhabi, the emirate's Civil Defence elsewhere)?
  - Is there a Civil Defence notice, observation list or deadline? Ask for it.
  - Building base-build system: tie into landlord's fire alarm and sprinklers? Landlord's approved contractor mandatory?
  - Maintenance: annual contract, number of visits, response time, Hassantuk connection in Dubai where required.
  - Ceiling type and height; working hours.
- **Photo/drawing tells you:** layout, ceiling, existing devices, panel make. **It cannot tell you:** code compliance, hazard classification, hydraulic capacity, panel spare capacity; Civil Defence approval and the approved contractor's engineer decide it.
- **Price drivers (spec decisions):** device counts; addressable vs conventional; panel capacity; sprinkler heads and pipework relocation; suppression agent and room volume; kitchen hood system; extinguisher count and types; emergency light count and battery duration; fire-rated doors; interface with access control and lifts; drawing preparation and Civil Defence submission/inspection; certificates; base-build landlord contractor requirement; maintenance visit frequency; working hours.
- **Misconceptions to correct:**
  - Only Civil Defence-approved companies may install or maintain fire systems; an unapproved firm puts the approval at risk.
  - Moving a sprinkler or detector during fit-out still needs approved drawings.
  - Extinguishers alone do not satisfy an inspection; the observation list sets the scope.
  - Maintenance is a legal obligation for the premises, not optional after handover.
  - "Normal cable will do for the fire alarm": no. Fire alarm and emergency circuits are wired in fire-resistant cable (standard or enhanced fire-resistant, low-smoke, e.g. FP200-class, as BS 5839-1 and the UAE Fire and Life Safety Code require and the approved drawings state). Civil Defence does not accept ordinary building cable, so it is not a saving; say so first, then ask the rest. (Ledger 1200 scenario bank, 27 Oct 2026.)
- **Complete quotable request =** emirate; premises type, area, floors, occupancy; systems and device counts; new/modify/maintenance; drawings status and who submits; Civil Defence notice or deadline; base-build tie-in and landlord contractor rule; ceiling type and height; maintenance scope; NOC/working hours; photos; timeline.
