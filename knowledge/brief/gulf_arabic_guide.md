# How UAE customers actually write: a reading guide for Gulf, Levantine and Egyptian Arabic

compiled 2026-10-13 by Claude for the customer_support and sales_engineer seats (founder ruling 1209)

**Purpose.** Customers rarely write Modern Standard Arabic. They write the way they speak: Emirati and wider
Gulf dialect, Levantine (Syrian, Lebanese, Jordanian, Palestinian), Egyptian, Arabizi (Arabic in Latin letters
with digits), and a constant mix of Arabic and English trade words. This guide is for UNDERSTANDING a request.
It is not a style to copy: our own Arabic replies stay in clear, polite, professional Arabic (fathatan written
before the alif, Arabic punctuation، ؟ ؛), and the brand stays in Latin letters. Suppliers are always written
to in English, whatever language the customer used.

**Rule of thumb.** Read for the job, not the spelling. A misspelt trade word is still a trade word. When one word
could mean two trades, ask one short question instead of guessing.

---

and otherwise explains that suppliers price it in their quotes.

**Words that point to two trades; ask before routing:**
- "صيانة" alone: general maintenance, AC maintenance or an AMC. Ask what needs maintaining.
- "رشاشات": garden irrigation sprinklers or fire sprinklers.
- "مظلات": car-park shades (metal) or fabric shades and pergolas (outdoor).
- "ديكور": gypsum, wall finishes, or full interior design.
- "مطبخ": kitchen cabinets (joinery), kitchen fit-out, or kitchen plumbing.
- "إنذار": intruder alarm (security) or fire alarm (fire safety, Civil Defence approved).
- "عزل": waterproofing or thermal insulation.
- "شبابيك" with "تسريب": the leak may be sealing (aluminium) or waterproofing.

## 6. Arabizi: Arabic in Latin letters

Digits stand for letters with no Latin equivalent:

| Digit | Letter | Example | Reads as |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | ء (hamza) | so2al | سؤال (question) |
| 3 | ع | 3ndi, 3ayez | عندي (I have), عايز (I want) |
| 3' or 8 | غ | 8ali, 3'ali | غالي (expensive) |
| 5 or 7' | خ | 5alas, 7'alas | خلاص (done, enough) |
| 6 | ط | 6ayeb | طيب (OK) |
| 7 | ح | 7ammam | حمام (bathroom) |
| 9 | ص | 9abbagh | صباغ (painter) |
| sh, ch | ش | shu, chu | شو (what) |

Typical Arabizi requests: "abgha a7ad ysalla7 el mukayyef" = I want someone to fix the AC. "bade 7ada yd7an el
shi2a" = I want someone to paint the apartment. "kam el 7ag" = what is the price. "mafi barid" = no cooling.
"el mai ytsarrab" = water is leaking. Treat the digits as letters, then read it as dialect.

## 7. Arabic and English mixed in one message

This is normal, not a sign of a confused customer. Common shapes:
- An English noun inside Arabic: "أبغى quotation حق gypsum ceiling للصالة" = I want a quotation for a gypsum
  ceiling in the living room.
- English trade words spelt in Arabic letters: سبليت، دكت، باركيه، فيت أوت، سبوت لايت، شاور بوكس، بريكر.
- Arabic particles on English words: "الـ AC خربان" = the AC is broken. "حق الـ villa" = for the villa.
- Units and sizes stay Latin: 120 sqm، 3 ton، 2.4 m، 60x60.
- Right-to-left text with numbers can display out of order on a phone. Re-read quantities and confirm them
  back to the customer before they reach a brief.

## 8. Faults and symptoms customers describe

| Written | Meaning |
|---|---|
| خربان / خربانة / عطلان / معطّل / بايظ | broken (Gulf / Levantine / Egyptian) |
| ما يشتغل / ما يشتغلش / مش شغال | does not work |
| ما يبرّد / ما فيه برودة / يطلع هوا حار | AC not cooling / blows hot air |
| يقطّر / يسرّب / يهرّب / ينقط | dripping / leaking |
| ريحة / ريحه / رائحة | smell (drain or mould) |
| رطوبة / عفن / فطريات | damp / mould |
| تشققات / شقوق / مشقّق | cracks |
| الكهرباء تطيح / يفصل / يطفي | the power trips / cuts out |
| صوت / إزعاج | noise |

A symptom is a strong clue to the trade: "the AC is dripping onto the ceiling" may need an AC technician AND a
gypsum repair. Name both in the brief rather than choosing one.

## 9. Politeness, register and what not to read into a message

- Short, direct messages ("كم الحق؟ أبغى صباغ فيلا") are polite in Gulf usage. Do not read them as rude, and
  do not answer at length.
- "إن شاء الله" with a date is a real intention, not a refusal. Confirm the date plainly.
- "يا أخوي / يا أخي / حبيبي / يا غالي" are friendly forms of address, not familiarity to mirror.
- Voice notes are common; their transcripts carry dialect spelling. Apply the same reading.
- A customer writing in Arabic is answered in Arabic; a customer writing in Arabizi or mixed text is answered
  in the language they used most. The brief and every message to a supplier are in English.

## 10. Quick reading checklist

1. Find the request verb (ابغى، بدي، عايز، محتاج) and the trade word, however it is spelt.
2. Find the place (villa, apartment, office) and the room.
3. Find size, quantity and timing (الحين، باجر، ضروري) and confirm numbers back.
4. If the trade word fits two trades (section 5), ask one short question.
5. Never correct the customer's spelling or dialect.
