# UAE contract practice primer: payments, retention, warranties and liability between customer and supplier

**Scope:** commercial terms that customers and suppliers in the UAE usually agree for construction, fit-out, MEP, joinery and maintenance work: advances, guarantees, retention, LPOs, payment terms, warranties, defects liability, decennial liability, variations, delay damages, FIDIC, VAT. It covers all 10 trade groups.

## 1. Advance (mobilisation) payments and advance-payment guarantees

- On larger projects the employer often pays an advance so the contractor can mobilise and buy materials. In return the contractor gives an **advance payment guarantee** (a bank guarantee, usually "on demand"). The guarantee normally reduces as the advance is recovered from interim payments. FIDIC 1999 deals with the advance in clause 14.2. Sources: https://mondaq.com/construction-planning/615376/legal-guide-to-construction-engineering-law ; DLA Piper, Chambers Real Estate guide https://gpg-pdf.chambers.com/view/133371374/1055
- A **performance bond** of about 10% of the contract price is common on formal contracts (DLA Piper, Chambers, above). The exact percentage on any given job is **(unverified)** and depends on the contract.
- On small residential and SME jobs there is usually no bank guarantee. The supplier simply asks for a cash, transfer or cheque advance against the quote or LPO **(unverified)**.

## 2. Retention

- Employers commonly withhold **retention**, often **10%** of each interim payment or of the contract price. Contractors sometimes give a retention bond instead. Source: Mondaq legal guide and DLA Piper (above).
- Release is usually split: **half at completion / handover** (taking-over) and **half when the defects liability period ends**. Source: Mondaq legal guide (above). A 5% retention or other splits also occur **(unverified)**.
- Small jobs (a few days of work, small supply-and-install) rarely use retention. The final instalment on completion serves the same purpose **(unverified)**.

## 3. LPOs (local purchase orders)

An LPO is the buyer's written order that accepts a supplier's quote. In UAE trade it is commonly treated as the document that confirms the deal **(unverified)**. A good LPO usually states:
- the buyer's and supplier's legal names and trade licence details, plus the TRN where VAT-registered
- a reference to the quote number and its date
- the scope, item list, quantities, specifications, brands or models, and drawings
- the price, currency, whether VAT is included, and the payment terms (advance %, milestones, credit days)
- the delivery or completion date and site address, who handles access, permits and NOCs, and any delay terms
- the warranty, defects period, retention (if any), and acceptance or handover criteria
- signature and company stamp

A practical point **(unverified)**: if the quote and the LPO disagree, say so before work starts. Ask for one written version that both sides sign.

## 4. Payment terms

- **Milestone payments.** Small and mid-size jobs often use an advance, then progress stages (for example on delivery, on installation, on completion), then a final payment after snagging **(unverified)**.
- **Interim / progress certificates.** On formal contracts the contractor applies for interim payment, usually monthly. An engineer or consultant certifies it, the employer pays within the agreed period, and retention is deducted. This is the FIDIC model (clause 14) **(unverified as to typical periods)**.
- **Credit days.** B2B supply invoices are often on 30, 60 or 90 days. Subcontractors may face "pay when paid" wording **(unverified)**. Parties can agree any credit period. The Commercial Transactions Law governs commercial dealings between traders where the contract is silent. Source: Federal Decree-Law No. 50 of 2022 on the Commercial Transactions Law, https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1580 (exact URL **(unverified)**; search "Commercial Transactions" on uaelegislation.gov.ae).
- **Cheques / PDCs (background).** Post-dated cheques have long been common security for payment in the UAE. Since the 2022 Commercial Transactions Law reforms, a bounced cheque for insufficient funds is largely handled as a civil and enforcement matter rather than a crime. Treat the details as **(unverified)**. Agents should not advise on cheque disputes.

## 5. VAT

- UAE VAT standard rate is **5%**. A VAT-registered supplier's tax invoice must show its TRN and the VAT charged. Source: Federal Tax Authority https://tax.gov.ae/en/taxes/vat.aspx ; https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/finance-and-investment/taxation/valueaddedtaxvat
- Quotes should state whether prices **include or exclude VAT**. A supplier that is not VAT-registered must not charge VAT.

## 6. Warranties and the defects liability period

- **Defects liability period (DLP)** / FIDIC "Defects Notification Period". It is commonly **12 months** from handover. During it the contractor must return and fix defects, and the second half of retention is held until it ends. Source for the DLP and retention link: Mondaq and DLA Piper (above). The 12-month figure is **(unverified)**.
- **Manufacturer warranty vs installer warranty.** Equipment (AC units, pumps, appliances, panels, hardware) carries the manufacturer's warranty, which usually needs proper installation and registration. Workmanship carries the installer's warranty, which is often shorter. Both should be written in the quote or LPO **(unverified)**.
- Specialist items such as waterproofing, roofing membranes, glazing sealants and anti-termite treatment often carry long written warranties (for example 5 or 10 years) from the applicator or manufacturer **(unverified)**.

## 7. Decennial (10-year) liability

- Under the **UAE Civil Transactions Law (Federal Law No. 5 of 1985)**, **Article 880** makes the **contractor and the architect/engineer jointly liable for 10 years** from handover. It covers total or partial collapse of buildings or fixed installations, and defects that threaten their stability or safety. Commentators describe it as strict liability that covers major structural defects, not ordinary snags.
- **Article 882**: any agreement that excludes or limits this liability is void.
- **Article 883**: claims must be brought within **3 years** of the collapse or of discovering the defect. Courts have read this as running from the claimant's knowledge.
- Sources: Al Tamimi, "Decennial liability in the UAE: a commentary", https://tamimi.com/law-update-articles/decennial-liability-in-the-uae-a-commentary ; Al Tamimi case note, https://turtl.tamimi.com/story/law-update-issue-366-transport-and-insurance/page/35 ; Mondaq, https://www.mondaq.com/real-estate/272284/limitations-on-liability-in-the-uae-beware
- **Amendment status:** Federal Decree-Law No. 30 of 2020 amended parts of the Civil Transactions Law. This compilation did **not** confirm whether it changed Articles 880-883. Treat the current wording as **(unverified)** and check the official text at https://uaelegislation.gov.ae
- The construction-contract (muqawala) chapter of the Civil Code (around Articles 872-896) also governs variations and price **(unverified range)**.
- Relevance: this mainly matters for structural, envelope and civil work. It rarely applies to furniture, finishes or maintenance.

## 8. Variations / change orders

- Changes to scope, quantity or specification after the LPO should be agreed **in writing before the work**, with the price and time effect stated. FIDIC handles this through the engineer's variation instruction (clause 13).
- Under the Civil Code, a contractor on a fixed lump-sum price generally cannot claim more for changes the owner did not authorise **(unverified)**. Disputes over unwritten "extras" are common, which is why written change orders matter **(unverified)**.

## 9. Delays and liquidated damages

- Formal contracts usually set **liquidated damages (LDs)** per day or week of delay, capped at about 10% of the contract value **(unverified)**.
- UAE courts can adjust agreed damages so they match the actual loss (Civil Transactions Law, Article 390). Source: Al Tamimi and Mondaq commentary (above, **(unverified)** as to article number in this compilation).
- Extensions of time usually apply for owner-caused delay, variations, and permit or authority delays outside the contractor's control **(unverified)**.

## 10. FIDIC in the UAE

- FIDIC forms are widely used in the UAE, mostly the **Red Book** (employer's design, measured or re-measured construction) and the **Yellow Book** (design-build / plant). Both 1999 and 2017 editions appear, almost always with heavy bespoke amendments. Source: Mondaq legal guide (above). The Yellow Book's share is **(unverified)**.
- Mandatory Civil Code provisions such as decennial liability override any contract clause that conflicts with them.
- Small residential and SME jobs almost never use FIDIC. The supplier's quote, its terms and conditions, and the LPO form the whole contract **(unverified)**.

## Typical norms per trade group

All figures below are market practice **(unverified)**, apart from the decennial and VAT points sourced above. Terms are always set between the customer and the supplier.

| Trade group | Typical commercial norms |
|---|---|
| **electrical_power_and_smart** | Small jobs: 50% advance, 50% on completion. Supply of panels or smart devices: higher advance because the stock is ordered in. Manufacturer warranty on devices, plus 6-12 months workmanship. Authority (DEWA etc.) inspection may be a payment milestone **(unverified)**. |
| **envelope_and_civil** | Formal contracts: advance with guarantee, monthly interim certificates, 10% retention, 12-month DLP. **Decennial liability applies** (Art. 880). Waterproofing often carries a 5-10 year written warranty **(unverified)**. |
| **flooring_and_stone** | Material-heavy work: 50-70% advance for stone, parquet or vinyl orders. Balance on laying or polishing. Workmanship warranty is usually short, 6-12 months **(unverified)**. |
| **hvac_plumbing_and_mep** | Projects: milestones (supply, installation, testing and commissioning), retention, 12-month DLP. Equipment under the manufacturer warranty (compressors often longer). Small repairs: paid on completion **(unverified)**. |
| **interiors_and_finishes** | Fit-out: 30-50% advance, then progress stages, final payment after snagging. Retention is possible on commercial fit-outs. Painting and gypsum: often 50/50 **(unverified)**. |
| **joinery_and_furniture** | Custom joinery: **50-60% advance** because material is bought and work is made to order, then the balance on delivery or installation. Hardware carries the manufacturer warranty; carcass and workmanship often 1-2 years **(unverified)**. |
| **low_current_and_safety** | Fire alarm / CCTV / access control: milestones linked to Civil Defence approval. Retention on projects. Fire systems usually need an annual maintenance contract **(unverified)**. |
| **metal_glass_and_structures** | Fabrication: 40-60% advance for steel or glass ordering. Balance on erection. Structural steel and glazing on buildings can fall under **decennial liability**. Sealant and glass warranties come from the manufacturer **(unverified)**. |
| **outdoor_and_pools** | Pools and landscaping: staged payments (excavation, shell, finishes, handover). Pool structure often has a long written warranty, equipment carries the manufacturer warranty. Irrigation or planting: short plant-survival warranties **(unverified)**. |
| **services_and_maintenance** | Annual maintenance contracts (AMCs) billed **quarterly or annually in advance**. One-off services (cleaning, pest control, repairs) paid on completion. Pest control often comes with a re-treatment warranty **(unverified)**. |
