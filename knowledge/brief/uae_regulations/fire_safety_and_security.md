# UAE fire & life safety and security systems: regulatory digest

- **Scope:** trade categories `safety` (fire & life safety systems: fire alarms, sprinklers, suppression, extinguishers, emergency lighting, fire doors) and `security` (CCTV, access control, intrusion alarms).
- **Compiled:** 2026-10-12 by Claude (it_engineer session) for the sales_engineer trade digest.
- **Rule for agents:** cite this as general guidance; the authority's current text governs.
- **Conventions:** every source was accessed on 2026-10-12. Anything marked "(unverified)" comes from secondary or commercial sources only. Do not state it to a customer as fact. Clause numbers are given only where the source names them.

## 1. Fire & life safety

### 1.1 Governing code and authorities

| Item | What is verified | Source |
|---|---|---|
| National code | The **UAE Fire and Life Safety Code of Practice**. DCD publishes the **September 2018** edition as a PDF; the DCD page is dated 24 Mar 2025. | https://www.dcd.gov.ae/portal/preventive-safety/uae-fire-and-life-safety-code-of-practice.jsp |
| Dubai authority | **Dubai Civil Defence (DCD)**. Its online services include "Company Approval", "Engineering Drawings" approval and "Hassantuk". | https://www.dcd.gov.ae/ ; https://smartservices.dcd.gov.ae/en |
| Abu Dhabi authority | **Abu Dhabi Civil Defence Authority (ADCDA)**. It requires installation and maintenance by companies that Civil Defence licenses, done by registered technicians and engineers who have passed the required tests. | https://www.adpolice.gov.ae/en/Media-Center/News/2021/07/01/-Civil-Defense-Abu-Dhabi--urges-building-owners-to-adopt-maintenance-contracts-for-prevention-and-sa |
| Federal basis | Cabinet Resolution No. 24 of 2012 on organising civil defence services. ADCDA cites **clause 91** of it for the AED 50,000 fine below. Reported by press, not read in the resolution itself. | https://gulfnews.com/uae/emergencies/abu-dhabi-civil-defence-dh50000-fine-for-unlicensed-fire-safety-activities-1.103754127 |
| Other emirates | Each emirate's Civil Defence applies the UAE Code. Sharjah, Ajman, RAK, UAQ and Fujairah procedures were not researched. (unverified) | n/a |

### 1.2 Approvals installers and products must hold

- **Contractor approval (Dubai).** Fire-system companies need DCD "Company Approval". Source: https://smartservices.dcd.gov.ae/en
  - A trade licence alone does not let a company design, install, test or maintain fire systems. (unverified, business-setup blog: https://www.businessdubai.ae/blogs/fire-safety-company-setup-dubai)
  - Approval also depends on a qualified fire-protection engineer who has passed DCD exams. (unverified, same blog)
- **Contractor approval (Abu Dhabi).**
  - Installation and maintenance must be done by Civil Defence-licensed companies, with registered and tested technicians. Source: ADPolice link in 1.1.
  - Manufacturing, trading, installing or maintaining fire safety systems without Civil Defence approval carries an **AED 50,000 fine**, as reported by press. Source: Gulf News link in 1.1.
- **Maintenance contract (Abu Dhabi).**
  - Owners must keep a maintenance contract with an approved contractor. Source: ADPolice link in 1.1.
  - The penalty for having no valid contract is reported as **AED 10,000**. (unverified, secondary: https://www.lexis.ae/2023/07/26/abu-dhabi-fire-safety-fine-reminder/)
- **Firestopping (Abu Dhabi, 2025 circular).** Specialty firestop contractors need **FM 4991 Approved** or **UL Qualified Firestop Contractor** certification. Suppliers that issue engineering judgments need the UL Technical Evaluation Developer Program. Source: copy of the ADCDA circular hosted by FCIA: https://www.fcia.org/Portals/0/2025%20ADCDA%20Circular%20Firestop%20and%20Through%20Penetration%20Systems%20%281%29.pdf
- **Product approval.** Fire products (detection, suppression, emergency lighting and others) are certified under the UAE Code before they may be used.
  - Certification goes through Civil Defence-recognised certification bodies or labs, such as the Emirates Safety Laboratory. (unverified, BSI exporter guide and press: https://www.bsigroup.com/globalassets/localfiles/en-in/resources/exporters_uae_civil_defence_web.pdf ; https://gulfnews.com/uae/dubai-announces-new-fire-safety-standards-1.95253431)
  - Buyer action: ask the supplier for the product's Civil Defence certificate or listing for the emirate where the work is done.
- **Combustible and insulation materials (Dubai).** A Dubai Municipality-hosted circular says materials such as rock wool, polystyrene and bitumen may be used only with DCD approval and per the latest UAE Code. Source: https://www.dm.gov.ae/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/5-1-1-Concerning-Fire-Prevention.pdf
- **MoIAT ECAS/EQM.** This is the federal conformity scheme for regulated products: a certificate valid for 1 year, specific to the product and its factory.
  - No source confirmed that extinguishers or detectors fall under ECAS. Treat Civil Defence approval as the controlling requirement for fire products. (unverified)
  - Sources: https://www.tuvsud.com/en-ae/services/product-certification/moiat-ecas-product-certification-services-in-uae ; https://www.sgs.com/en-ae/news/2024/06/pca-2024-q2-navigating-uae-product-conformity-understanding-ecas-and-eqm-certification
- **Hassantuk.** A DCD-listed service for connected fire alarm monitoring. Which premises it applies to was not verified. Source: https://smartservices.dcd.gov.ae/en (scope unverified)

### 1.3 Thresholds and limits

- Sprinkler, detection and emergency-lighting thresholds (building height, area, occupancy), and fire-rating durations for doors and walls, are set in the 2018 Code text. They were **not extracted for this digest**.
- Do not quote figures such as "60 min door" or "sprinklers above X m" as regulation. Refer the buyer to the consultant's DCD/ADCDA-approved drawings. (unverified)

## 2. Security systems (CCTV, access control, intrusion)

### 2.1 Governing law and authority

| Item | What is verified | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Dubai regulator | **SIRA**, the Security Industry Regulatory Agency. It regulates the private security sector in Dubai, including security systems, and keeps a directory of licensed companies. | https://www.sira.gov.ae/ |
| Dubai law | **Law No. (12) of 2016** Regulating the Security Industry in the Emirate of Dubai. It defines "Preventive Systems" (electronic systems and security equipment). | https://dlp.dubai.gov.ae/Legislation%20Reference/2016/Law%20No.%20(12)%20of%202016.html |
| Implementing bylaw | Board of Directors **Resolution No. (1) of 2018**. It specifies which establishments must have systems. | https://dlp.dubai.gov.ae/Legislation%20Reference/2018/Resolution%20No.%20(1)%20of%202018.html |
| Technical standard | **Administrative Resolution No. (13) of 2025**, the **Preventive Systems Manual**. It was issued 14 Apr 2025 and took effect on Official Gazette publication (Article 4). It applies to the establishments in Resolution 1/2018 (Article 2) and sets minimum specs for CCTV, access control, alarms, locks, glass and vehicle barriers, plus sector rules for hotels, malls, banks, data centres and others. | https://dlp.dubai.gov.ae/Legislation%20Reference/2025/Administrative%20Resolution%20No.%20(13)%20of%202025%20the%20Preventive%20System%20Manual.html |
| Abu Dhabi and other emirates | Security-systems licensing outside Dubai (Abu Dhabi Police / Monitoring & Control Centre and others) was not researched. (unverified) | n/a |

### 2.2 Approvals and obligations (Dubai, Preventive Systems Manual, same source as above)

- **Maintenance contract.** The establishment must hold an **annual maintenance contract with a SIRA-registered company**, including preventive maintenance. It must tell SIRA if the contractor does not perform.
- **Technician licensing.** The whole technical maintenance crew must be **licensed by SIRA**, and providers must keep their crew details up to date with SIRA.
- **Change notification.** Any change to the system, its layout or the premises, however minor, must be notified to SIRA and recorded.
- **Deviations.** Anything below the Manual's minimum specs needs official SIRA approval.
- **Recorders.** Recording devices must be kept in secure, monitored locations with an auditable access log.
- **Warning signs.** SIRA-approved CCTV warning signs are required at main entrances and inside lifts.
- **Wireless components.** Wireless sensors and alarm components generally need prior SIRA approval.
- **Company licence.** Security-system installers and maintainers need a SIRA licence; the SIRA site has a company directory.
  - Reported fees of AED 220–520 per activity are unverified (consultancy blog: https://www.rizmona.com/blog/sira-license-dubai-uae/).
  - A reported one-year accreditation period is also unverified.

### 2.3 Key numbers (Dubai, Preventive Systems Manual)

- CCTV recordings must be kept for **at least 31 days**, at high quality and **at least 10 frames per second**.
- Hotel door-lock systems must keep **at least six months** of movement records.
- The "30–90 days storage" figure seen on blogs is **not** the rule. Use 31 days minimum, plus any sector-specific rule in the Manual.

## 3. Glossary (brief)

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| DCD / ADCDA | Dubai Civil Defence / Abu Dhabi Civil Defence Authority: the fire-safety approval authorities |
| UAE FLS Code | UAE Fire and Life Safety Code of Practice (DCD publishes the Sept 2018 edition) |
| FACP | Fire alarm control panel |
| Addressable vs conventional | Addressable: each detector reports its own address. Conventional: the panel reports by zone only |
| Clean agent / FM-200 / Novec / CO2 | Gaseous suppression systems, typically for server and electrical rooms |
| Wet / dry / pre-action / deluge | Sprinkler system types |
| FRL / fire rating (e.g. 60/90/120 min) | How long a door, wall or damper resists fire, per its test certificate |
| Firestopping | Sealing of penetrations through fire-rated walls and floors |
| AMC | Annual maintenance contract |
| SIRA | Security Industry Regulatory Agency (Dubai) |
| Preventive Systems | Legal term (Dubai Law 12/2016) for security systems and equipment |
| NVR/DVR, VMS | Network/digital video recorder; video management software |
| ACS / ANPR | Access control system; automatic number-plate recognition |
| ECAS / EQM | MoIAT federal product conformity certificate / quality mark |

## 4. What a buyer should check in a quote

1. **Fire works.** The contractor's current Civil Defence approval for the emirate (DCD Company Approval, or ADCDA licence) and the approval category that matches the scope (install, maintain, firestop).
2. **Security works in Dubai.** The company appears in SIRA's licensed directory, and its technicians hold SIRA licences.
3. **Products.** Make, model and Civil Defence certificate or listing for every fire item (detectors, panels, sprinkler heads, extinguishers, fire doors, emergency lights). For electrical items, check ECAS/EQM where it applies.
4. **Fire doors and firestopping.** The tested fire rating in minutes, the certificate, and installation per the tested system. In Abu Dhabi, FM 4991 or UL QFC for firestop contractors.
5. **Drawings and approvals.** Who submits the drawings to DCD/ADCDA, who books inspections, and who obtains the completion or approval certificate. All of this should be priced in the quote.
6. **CCTV specs (Dubai).** Recording retention of at least 31 days, at least 10 fps, storage sized to match, secure recorder location, SIRA signage, and whether SIRA approval or notification is included.
7. **AMC.** Price and term of the annual maintenance contract, preventive-maintenance visit frequency, and response time. An AMC is mandatory for Dubai security systems and Abu Dhabi fire systems.
8. **Exclusions.** Civil works, power, network cabling, authority fees and re-inspection fees.
