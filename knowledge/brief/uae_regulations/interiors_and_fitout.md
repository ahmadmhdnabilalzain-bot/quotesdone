## 1. Which authority approves a fit-out

Jurisdiction depends on **where the unit is**, not on the type of business. Before quoting, a supplier should confirm which authority applies.

| Area | Approving body | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Most of mainland Dubai | Dubai Municipality (DM) fit-out / building-modification permit | Official DM permit portal not fetched here. The scope description ("partitions, ceilings, flooring, MEP, finishes") comes from secondary guides **(unverified)**. Source: https://www.dubizzle.com/blog/property/dubai-fit-out-approvals/ |
| DDA zones (e.g. Dubai Internet City, Media City, Design District, other TECOM clusters) | Dubai Development Authority (DDA) fit-out permit | Official page, see §2. https://dda.gov.ae/en/planning-development/construction/permits-nocs/fit-out-permit |
| Trakhees areas (Ports, Customs & Free Zone Corporation: e.g. JAFZA, and Nakheel communities such as Palm Jumeirah) | Trakhees (PCFC) Civil Engineering modification NOC/permit | The Trakhees licensing rules state that a Civil Engineering Department modification NOC/permit is mandatory before any modification or fit-out work. https://www.pcfc.ae/en/Rules%20and%20Regulations/Trakhees/Licensing%20Department/Licensing/Rules%20&%20Regulations/PCFC-TRK-LD-LS-REG02,%20Operational%20Requirements%20for%20Trakhees.pdf . The full list of Trakhees communities is **(unverified)**. |
| Nakheel master communities | Nakheel master-developer NOC / stamped drawings, which then go to Trakhees | No standalone "Nakheel fit-out permit" was found. Nakheel's role appears to be the NOC and drawing stamp that feed the Trakhees permit **(unverified)**. |
| Fire and life safety (all of Dubai) | Dubai Civil Defence (DCD) | DCD approval and completion are separate from the fit-out permit (see §2 and §3). |
| Other emirates | Each emirate's municipality, civil defence and free-zone authority | Not researched here. Do not apply Dubai rules to Abu Dhabi, Sharjah or the other emirates. |

**Typical sequence (general):** landlord or building-management NOC, then authority permit, then work starts, then inspections and a completion certificate. Work should not start before the permit is issued. Source for the sequence: secondary guides **(unverified as an official rule outside DDA and Trakhees)**.

**Contractor licensing:** Secondary sources mention a Dubai Contractor Register and contractor grading under "Dubai Law No. 7 of 2025" from January 2026. **(unverified: not confirmed against an official text.)** Source: https://www.businessdubai.ae/blogs/interior-fit-out-company-dubai . Interior design consultancy and fit-out contracting are usually separate licence activities **(unverified)**.

## 2. DDA fit-out permit (official page)

Source: https://dda.gov.ae/en/planning-development/construction/permits-nocs/fit-out-permit (accessed 2026-10-12)

- **Documents:** permit request form (customer-service-centre route only); contractor appointment letter issued by the tenant; EJARI copy; NOC from the building owner; NOCs from relevant authorities (per DDA's NOC matrix); approved stamped drawings and proposed drawings per DDA "Circular No. 400".
- **Conditions:**
  - A Dubai Civil Defence completion certificate is required before the fit-out completion certificate is issued.
  - Coring under 200 mm must not affect structural elements such as tendons and must be at least 1 m from a column.
  - Adding structural elements, changing the building's use, or adding gross floor area requires a building modification request, not a fit-out permit. If DDA reclassifies a submission this way, the fit-out fees are not refunded.
  - If the building has no completion certificate yet, NOCs from the main contractor, main consultant and plot owner are also required.
- **Validity:** 6 months.
- A DDA circular (https://dda.gov.ae/-/media/116360B0DDBF4E1E89473C90A530CEAD.pdf) is reported to exempt minor works such as internal painting from the fit-out permit. **(unverified: the circular's text was not read.)**

## 3. Fire safety: materials, curtains and upholstery

- **Dubai Civil Defence circular "5-1-1 Concerning Fire Prevention" (2024 upload on dm.gov.ae):** it prohibits flammable materials, or materials with flammable components, unless the General Directorate of Civil Defence in Dubai has approved them under the latest **UAE Fire and Life Safety Code**. The examples it gives are insulation (rock wool, polystyrene) and fixing materials. The circular is addressed to consultants, contractors, suppliers and developers. https://www.dm.gov.ae/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/5-1-1-Concerning-Fire-Prevention.pdf
- **Curtains, drapes, blinds, upholstery and carpets:** no Dubai text was found that names these items. They fall under the general rule above. The specific test classes the UAE Fire and Life Safety Code requires for interior textiles and finishes were **not verified** (the code text was not read). Practical guidance for agents:
  - For commercial, hospitality and public spaces, buyers usually ask for **flame-retardant (FR) certified fabric** with a test report and DCD-acceptable certification. Agents should ask the supplier to state the standard tested to (e.g. NFPA 701 or BS 5867 for curtains; BS 5852 for upholstery) **(general industry knowledge; acceptance by DCD unverified)**.
  - Some DCD-regulated products must be certified via an approved laboratory. A news report names an "Emirates Safety Laboratory" **(unverified scope)**. https://gulfnews.com/uae/dubai-announces-new-fire-safety-standards-1.95253431
  - Where the UAE code is silent, DCD has reportedly fallen back on NFPA standards (news source, older) **(unverified)**. https://www.gulf-times.com/story/529977/dubai-toughens-fire-rules-after-tower-blazes
- **Fire-rated items in fit-out:** fire-rated doors, fire-rated gypsum partitions and ceiling systems, and fire-stopping normally need DCD-approved products, and the DCD approval should be listed in the quote. **(general industry practice; specific listing rules unverified)**

## 4. Paint and coatings: VOC limits (Dubai Al Sa'fat)

Dubai Municipality's green building system **Al Sa'fat** (2017 edition referenced) sets limits for low-emitting paints, coatings, adhesives and sealants. **Dubai Central Laboratory (DCL)** issues product conformity certificates against it. The figures below are **taken from DCL certificates** that quote the Al Sa'fat limits, not from the regulation text. Check the current Al Sa'fat edition before relying on them.

| Product subcategory (as stated on certificates) | Max VOC |
|---|---|
| Interior matt walls/ceilings (gloss < 25 @ 60°) | 30 g/L |
| Exterior walls, mineral substrate | 40 g/L |
| Multi-coloured water-based coatings | 100 g/L |
| One-pack water-based performance coatings | 140 g/L |
| Two-pack reactive / floor coatings (solvent-based) | 500 g/L |

The same certificates also show these limits: heavy metals (Cd, Cr VI < 500 mg/kg; As, Hg, Pb < 100 mg/kg) and formaldehyde (< 10 mg/kg).
Sources: https://www.dm.gov.ae/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/CL17020457.pdf ; https://www.dm.gov.ae/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/CL18020619.pdf ; https://www.dm.gov.ae/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/CL18020628.pdf

**Relevance to agents:** epoxy-flooring and two-pack floor coatings fall in the 500 g/L band. Buyers on green-rated or government projects may ask for a **DCL certificate** for paint, adhesives (tiling, flooring, wallpaper) and sealants.

## 5. Federal product conformity (MoIAT / ECAS)

- The **Ministry of Industry and Advanced Technology (MoIAT)** runs **ECAS** (Emirates Conformity Assessment Scheme, mandatory for regulated products, certificate valid 1 year) and **EQM** (Emirates Quality Mark, certificate valid 3 years). Regulated products need one of these to be sold in the UAE. https://www.sgs.com/en-ae/news/2024/06/pca-2024-q2-navigating-uae-product-conformity-understanding-ecas-and-eqm-certification
- **Paints and varnishes** are certified under ECAS against the technical requirements of **Cabinet Resolution No. 6 of 2016**, as described by a certification body. Whether this resolution is current or has been amended is **(unverified)**. https://cdn3-verigates.bureauveritas.com/sites/verigates/files/2023-02/AE%20-%20MoIAT%20Service%20Cards%2009022023.pdf
- New ECAS logos and conformity marks were set by **Ministerial Resolution No. 137 of 2024**, with a 6-month transition for new imports and 1 year for existing stock. https://www.sgs.com/en-iq/news/2024/11/moiat-announces-new-logo-and-conformity-marks-under-ministerial-resolution-no-137-of-2024
- **Wood-based panels (MDF, particleboard, plywood):** no UAE regulation setting a formaldehyde emission class was found. A certification body's MoIAT service list includes a "Wood Materials" category, but its requirements were not found. **(unverified: do not state a UAE-mandated E-class.)**

## 6. Materials primer (general industry knowledge)

These are not UAE regulations. Use them to explain options to buyers.

**Wood panels (carpentry-joinery, kitchens, custom-furniture)**
- **MDF (medium-density fibreboard):** made from fine wood fibres and resin. Smooth and uniform, it machines and paints well (good for lacquered doors and CNC routing). It is heavy, holds screws poorly at the edges, and swells if water gets in.
- **MR MDF (moisture-resistant, often green-cored):** has better swell resistance and is used for kitchens, vanities and humid areas. It is **not waterproof**: edges must be sealed.
- **Particleboard / chipboard:** made from wood chips and resin. It is the cheapest option and is usually supplied melamine-faced (MFC) for carcasses. It has the weakest screw holding and moisture tolerance.
- **Plywood:** cross-laminated veneers. It has the best strength-to-weight ratio and screw holding. Marine or WBP grades use water-boil-proof glue for wet areas. Its surface is less smooth than MDF for paint.
- **Formaldehyde emission classes:** **E1** (EN 13986 / EN 717-1 chamber test, ≤ 0.1 ppm, about 0.124 mg/m³) is the European baseline. **E0** and **"super E0"** are lower industry or market labels (commonly cited for Asian and Chinese grades). **CARB Phase 2 / TSCA Title VI** is the US limit regime. Ask for the test certificate, not just the label.
  Sources: https://www.epa.gov/formaldehyde/formaldehyde-emission-standards-composite-wood-products ; https://ww2.arb.ca.gov/our-work/programs/composite-wood-products-program

**Tiles and stone (tiling, flooring, marble-granite)**
- **Porcelain vs ceramic:** porcelain is fired denser, with water absorption **≤ 0.5%** (ISO 13006 group BIa). It is harder and frost-resistant, suits floors, wet areas and outdoors, and is harder to cut. Ceramic is more porous (higher absorption) and is usually used on walls and for light-traffic floors. Full-body porcelain shows less wear on chips than glazed porcelain. Source: https://www.iso.org/standard/60268.html
- **Slip resistance:** buyers may ask for an R-rating (DIN 51130, R9 to R13) or a pendulum (PTV) value for wet areas and exteriors.
- **Granite:** a natural igneous stone. It is very hard and resistant to heat and scratching, and should be sealed periodically.
- **Marble:** a softer calcareous stone. It **etches with acids** (lemon, vinegar, some cleaners) and stains, needs sealing, and can be honed or polished.
- **Quartz (engineered stone):** about 90%+ crushed quartz bound with resin. It is non-porous with no sealing needed and gives consistent patterns. It is **less heat-tolerant** (resin can scorch, so use trivets) and is not UV-stable outdoors. Fabrication involves respirable crystalline silica, so cutting needs dust control and wet methods. Source: https://www.osha.gov/silica-crystalline
- **Sintered stone / porcelain slabs:** heat- and UV-resistant and very hard. Edges can chip.

**Other trades**
- **Gypsum board:** standard; moisture-resistant (MR, usually green) for bathrooms and kitchens; fire-rated (FR, usually pink or red, type X). The fire-resistance rating comes from the **tested system** (studs, layers, insulation), not from the board alone. **(general industry knowledge)**
- **Epoxy flooring:** comes as a self-levelling, roller-coat or mortar screed system. It needs a dry, primed substrate, so the concrete moisture test should be stated in the quote. PU and PU-cement systems handle heat and thermal shock better (kitchens). **(general industry knowledge)**
- **Carpets:** the main choice is broadloom vs carpet tile. Nylon is resilient and polypropylene is cheaper and stain-resistant. For commercial spaces, ask for fire classification and use class. **(general industry knowledge)**
- **Wallpaper:** vinyl is washable and suits commercial use. In humid UAE interiors, wall moisture and mould-resistant adhesive matter. Commercial projects may also require fire classification. **(general industry knowledge)**

## 7. What an agent should ask a buyer before quoting (checklist)

1. Location and authority: DM, DDA, Trakhees/Nakheel, another free zone, or another emirate.
2. Who obtains the NOC and permit: the buyer or the contractor (include this in scope and price).
3. Is it commercial or public space (FR fabric and DCD-approved materials likely needed) or a private villa or apartment?
4. Required certificates: DCL low-VOC, ECAS, FR test reports, panel E-class.
5. Wet-area specifications: MR MDF or plywood, porcelain, slip rating.
