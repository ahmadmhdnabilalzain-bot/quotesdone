# Liability exclusion — the legal basis (Supplier Agreement v15, Customer Terms v10)

Seat: ceo. Founder rulings: ledger 1155 (suppliers), 1156 (customers), addendum "just make sure we are protected in all cases".
Researched 6 Oct 2026. Every article marked VERIFIED was read today on the official site uaelegislation.gov.ae
(English text; the site states the Arabic text prevails). Read through the founder's browser session because
direct HTTP fetches of the site return 403 (Cloudflare).

## 0. The most important finding: the Civil Code has been replaced

Federal Law 5/1985 (the old Civil Transactions Law) is REPEALED. Federal Decree-Law No. 25 of 2025 (Civil
Transactions Law) has been in force since 1 June 2026 (https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/4011).
The often-quoted "Art. 296 (exemption from harmful-act liability void)" refers to the OLD numbering. The same rule
is now **Art. 257**. Drafting cites only the new numbers.

## 3. How the drafts use this — strength of each protection

| Protection | Strength | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Not a party to the customer–supplier contract; no liability for its performance, the quote, value, quality, delays, defects, payment between them, solvency | **Strong** | CTL Art. 265(1) says exactly this for a broker; Art. 2(1) freedom of contract. Lost only if WE committed fraud or fault (Arts. 258, 265(1)) or took an interest beyond our fee (265(2)). |
| Not liable for a supplier's statements, misrepresentation or lies | **Strong**, subject to our own fault | A supplier's lie is the supplier's act. A court could find fault on our side only if we knew or should have known and did nothing (Art. 258 duty to disclose circumstances known to us). Keep vetting/flagging processes real. |
| No liability to suppliers at all (v15 clause 18) | **Medium** | Valid between merchants (CTL Art. 2(1); Civil Transactions Law Art. 113(2)) but the agreement is adhesion (CivL 223): a court may trim a total exclusion as unfair. Fallback cap (greater of 12-month fees or AED 5,000) is built for that case. |
| Fallback caps (supplier: greater of 12-month fees or AED 5,000; customer: the greater of Reveal Fees paid in the 12 months or AED 1,000) | **Medium–strong** | A cap is a mitigation; valid for contractual liability, void for harmful-act liability (Art. 257) and fraud/gross fault (Art. 334(2)); consumer damage claims under CPL Art. 24 are outside it. |
| No warranty on availability, matching, AI comparisons/estimates ("information, not advice") | **Strong** for business users; **medium** for consumers | Disclaimer of a result not promised; CPL Art. 21 keeps our statutory obligations to consumers. |
| Third-party services (Stripe, Microsoft, Twilio, Meta, hosting) | **Strong** for their own failures; not for our choice of them if grossly negligent | Not our act; Art. 334(2) carve-out. |
| Force majeure | **Strong** | CivL Art. 236 already gives it; clause defines examples. |
| Indemnity from suppliers and business customers | **Strong** between merchants | Freedom of contract; not imposed on consumers. |
| Claims-notice period (90 days) | **Weak if drafted as a bar — so it is NOT** | CivL Art. 443(1) voids an agreed shorter limitation period. Drafted as a notice obligation; late notice never bars the claim, it only stops recovery of loss that prompt notice would have avoided. |
| Suspend / remove | **Strong** | contractual right, exercised in good faith (Art. 221). |
| Entire agreement | **Strong** for prior talks; cannot oust fraud or mandatory law | Art. 113(2). |

## 4. Carve-outs kept in both instruments (the law forces them)

Fraud; gross negligence / gross fault (CivL 334(2)); liability for a harmful act (CivL 257); death or personal
injury; our own fault as broker (CTL 258, 265(1)); every non-waivable consumer right (CPL 21, 24); any
liability that UAE law does not allow to be limited or excluded.
