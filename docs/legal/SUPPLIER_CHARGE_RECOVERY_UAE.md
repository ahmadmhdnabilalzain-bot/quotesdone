## 1. Answer in one paragraph

An unpaid non-response charge is a **civil/commercial debt**. It goes to the **Dubai Courts as a payment order**
(أمر أداء, "writ of debt" in the official English), **not to Dubai Police**. Before filing, QuotesDone must serve a
**notice for payment giving at least 5 days**. QuotesDone gives 7. The notice may go by email, SMS, a smart app, or
any means the parties agreed. The judge decides within 3 business days, and the fee is AED 500 at these amounts. The
claim is **time-barred 5 years from the due date**. **9% is not a rate set by UAE law.** It is lawful here because
the parties agreed it: it runs as simple interest from the due date, and compound interest is prohibited.

## 3. Findings

### a) Courts, not police
- **CPL Art 143(1)** provides the payment-order route where *"the creditor's right is well-established in writing
  – whether electronically or in a paper format - and due for settlement"*, for *"a debt of money of a specified
  amount"*. That describes this charge.
- **No offence fits a plain unpaid contractual charge.**
  - Fraud (Penal Code Art 451) needs the victim to have been deceived into handing property over. QuotesDone hands
    the supplier nothing.
  - Breach of trust (Art 453) needs money entrusted to the supplier (deposit, agency and the like). The supplier
    never holds QuotesDone's money.
- **When the police are the venue:**
  - **A cheque.** Only if a supplier pays by cheque and commits one of the remaining cheque offences (CTL Arts
    674–676: stopping payment or closing the account, among others).
  - **Fraud.** Only if the supplier registered through genuine deception.
- **Say it plainly: Dubai Police are not the venue for an unpaid invoice.**
  - This is a reading of the texts above. **Not verified:** no Dubai Police page stating it was read.

### b) The payment order (CPL Arts 143–148)
- **Conditions (Art 143(1)).** The debt must be in writing (electronic is expressly allowed), due, and of a
  specified amount.
  - Art 143(3): filing *"shall not preclude the petitioner from claiming the interests or damages"*.
- **Prior notice (Art 144(1)).** *"The creditor shall first serve upon the debtor a notice for payment within at
  least five [5] days."*
  - The notice must claim **no less** than the petition: *"The right described in the notice for payment shall not
    be less than the right described in the petition."*
  - The notice is served *"by any of the means of service defined in this Code"*.
  - Venue: the debtor's domicile, or where the contract was made or performed. Either way it is Dubai, and clause
    34 gives the Dubai courts exclusive jurisdiction.
- **The petition (Art 144(2)–(3)).**
  - It may be electronic or on paper.
  - It must be *"accompanied by a proof of the debt and a proof of serving a notice for payment upon the debtor"*.
  - It must carry the Art 44 particulars: both parties' names, identification, domicile, phone and email; the
    court; the date; the subject, relief and grounds; and a signature.
- **Decision and service.**
  - The order issues *"not later than [3] three business days following the filing date"* (Art 144(4)).
  - A dismissal must give reasons (Art 145).
  - The order is void if not served *"within [3] three months"* (Art 146(2)).
- **Challenge.**
  - The debtor may file a grievance *"within [15] fifteen days"* of service (Art 147(1)).
  - Up to the AED 50,000 final-judgment limit (Art 29(1)(b)), the grievance ruling is final. Every realistic
    non-response charge is below that limit.
- **Dubai Courts service C5, "Repayment Order".**
  - It lists: the statement of case and supporting documents; *"the Warning Notice with delivery notification"*;
    the trade licence.
  - All documents as PDF, and translated into Arabic by MOJ-approved legal translators.
  - Fee: *"6% of the value of the case… not less than AED 500 and not more than AED (40,000)"*. A claim of a few
    thousand dirhams therefore pays **AED 500**.
  - Filed on the website after registering, or at a service centre.
- **Filing without a lawyer (CPL Art 52(1)).**
  - A litigant may appear *"in person or through an attorney"*.
  - A private legal person may instead act through an employee holding a notarised power of attorney.
  - QuotesDone is a **sole establishment**, so the founder is himself the litigant and files in person. That
    reading is **not verified** with Dubai Courts.
- **Language.** *"Arabic shall be the official language of courts"* (CPL Art 5(1)). The documents here are drafted
  in Arabic. From Supplier Agreement v11 the Arabic text prevails for all purposes, including before the courts
  (clause 22), so the agreement is filed in its own Arabic text; any other document not in Arabic needs an
  MOJ-approved translation. (Under v10 the English text governed and needed a translation.)

### c) Serving the notice
- **CPL Art 9(1)(a)** permits *"Audio or video recorded call, SMS to mobile phone, smart applications, email, fax,
  other modern means of communication or by any other means to be agreed upon by the parties"*.
  - Art 9(2): the channel must be *"personally relating to the Defendant"*.
  - Art 11(3): service takes effect from the sending date of the email or text message.
- **CTL Art 81** for commercial notices: through the notary, by registered letter with acknowledgement, by an
  electronic means set by Ministry of Justice resolution, *"or by any other method as agreed between the parties"*.
- **What this build does.** The final demand goes three ways, and the provider message ids are kept as proof of
  service:
  - to the **registered email** — agreed in clause 21;
  - to the **supplier portal** — agreed in clause 21;
  - by **WhatsApp/SMS to the registered mobile** — a CPL Art 9 means.
- **A notarised notice (إنذار عدلي) is optional, not required.**
  - Dubai Courts G5 "Legal Notices" costs AED 200 for attestation plus AED 100 per signature for data entry. It is
    available online with an Emirates ID.
  - It is the strongest proof of service. Use it when a supplier later disputes receipt of an electronic notice.
  - How G5 delivers the notice to the recipient is **not verified**.

### d) Limitation
- **CTL Art 92.** Claims on merchants' obligations to each other are barred *"after the lapse of (5) five years
  from the date on which the performance of the obligation falls due"*. The bar applies where the debtor denies the
  debt.
  - It is Art 92, not 95.
  - The clock starts on the clause 33 due date: 7 days after the invoice.
- **The civil default is 15 years** under Civil Transactions Law 25/2025 Art 429 (formerly Art 473 of 5/1985).

### e) Interest — what the law says about 9%
- **CTL Art 72.** A contract rate applies. Where none is stated, the market rate applies, *"provided that it does not
  exceed (9%)"*. **So 9% is a ceiling for the no-agreement case, not "the legal rate".**
- **CTL Art 73.** Where a rate is stipulated and the debtor delays, *"the delay interest shall be calculated on the
  basis of the agreed rate until full repayment"*.
- **CTL Art 84.** Delay on *"a sum of money that was a sum certain at the time the obligation was created"* attracts
  that interest.
- **CTL Art 85.** No proof of damage is needed.
- **CTL Art 86.** Interest runs *"from the maturity date… unless otherwise provided by the Law or agreement"*.
- **CTL Art 88.** *"A creditor may not claim a compound interest"*.
- **No "interest may not exceed the principal" cap** appears in CTL Arts 70–89. Whether CPL 42/2022 contains any
  interest provision was **not verified**.
- **For the 9% to be claimable, the contract must state** the rate, that it is simple and per annum, that it is
  delay interest, and the due date it runs from. **Clauses 31 and 33 state all four** (see §6).
- **The accrual formula** (`supabase/functions/_shared/supplier_charge.ts`): `interest = unpaid principal × 9% ×
  days after the due date ÷ 365`. It is simple interest on (a)+(b) only, rounded to the fils.

### f) The AED 250 administrative fee
- **Civil Transactions Law 25/2025 Art 340**, verbatim from the official text
  (https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/4011; Arabic https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/ar/legislations/4011):
  - 340(1): *"The contracting parties may pre-determine the amount of compensation by stipulating it in the contract
    or in a subsequent agreement, subject to the provisions of the law."* Arabic: «يجوز للمتعاقدين أن يُحددا مقدمًا
    قيمة التعويض بالنص عليها في العقد أو في اتفاق لاحق، مع مراعاة أحكام القانون.»
  - 340(2): the court may reduce it *"if the debtor proves that the assessment was excessive or that the original
    obligation has been partially performed."*
  - 340(3): it may be reduced where the creditor's own fault contributed to the damage.
  - 340(4): more than the agreed amount only on proof of *"fraud or gross fault."*
  - 340(5): *"Any agreement contrary to the provisions of this Article shall be void."*
- **The documents cite 340(1)** for the AED 250, which clause 33 stipulates. The Arabic application quotes the
  official Arabic wording; nothing in it is a translation made here.
- **Transition (Art 4(1)).** The new law does not apply to acts before 1 June 2026. Every supplier acceptance of v10 or v11
  is after that date, so Art 340 governs.
- **Enforceability.** The AED 250 is enforceable as agreed, subject to the 340(2) reduction power. Its best defence
  is that clause 33 already ties it to QuotesDone's real cost of processing the refund.
- **A risk.** A judge may treat the AED 250, or the whole charge, as compensation rather than a debt. The amount is
  fixed only when the refund happens, which leaves room to argue it was not a "sum certain at creation" (CTL Art
  84). That could make a judge send the claim to an ordinary case instead of a payment order. See §8.

## 8. What the law does not settle — confirm before the first real filing
1. **Click-accept as "writing".** Whether Dubai Courts accepts the click-accept record (time, IP, hash) plus the
   refund ledger as a debt "well-established in writing". Art 143 allows electronic writing, but this has not been
   tested.
2. **"Sum certain" (CTL Art 84).** Whether a judge treats the charge as a fixed debt or as compensation. As
   compensation it would go to Art 340 review and possibly out of the payment-order route.
3. **The Amicable Settlement Centre.** Whether a claim under AED 500,000 must first go to the Centre (Dubai Courts
   Resolution 4/2025 Art 1(3)). The resolution does not mention payment orders.
4. **Proof of electronic service.** What C5 accepts as the *"Warning Notice with delivery notification"* for email
   and WhatsApp: a screenshot, a provider delivery receipt, or the outbox record.
5. **The fee.** C5 says 6%, minimum AED 500, maximum AED 40,000. Law 21/2015 Art 14(b) caps commercial filings under
   AED 500k at AED 5,000. At these amounts both give AED 500.
6. **Filing in person.** That the owner of a sole establishment files in person on the website without a power of
   attorney. Advocacy Law 34/2022 was not read.
7. **Translation.** Whether the Arabic drafts produced here can be filed as they are (they are drafted in Arabic,
   not translated), and which documents need an MOJ-approved translation. At minimum the English T&C does.
8. **Cabinet Resolution 57/2018.** Whether this executive regulation, or a newer one, still applies alongside CPL
   42/2022.
9. **Dubai Police.** Confirm that a plain civil debt is not registered as a police case. No primary page was read.
10. **Local notary rules.** How a G5 notarised notice is delivered, and which Dubai notary legislation governs it
   (Decree-Law 20/2022 Art 2 excludes local judicial authorities).
