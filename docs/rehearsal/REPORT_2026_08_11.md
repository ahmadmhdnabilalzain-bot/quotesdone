## 1. CONTAINMENT, BUILT AND PROVEN BEFORE THE FIRST SEED ROW

19,903 leads carry a real, unbanned email address and **45 SQL routines insert into
`notifications`** — four of them fan out over matched leads. One table, so one choke point:

`zzz_notifications_demo_guard`, a BEFORE INSERT trigger. While
`decision_policy.demo_rehearsal_active` is true, **every** recipient that is not a reserved
`.invalid` address is set to `held`. Not "every demo-derived recipient" — every recipient. Tracing
"arising from a demo entity" would have to walk `lead_id`/`inquiry_id`/`quote_id` back on each
insert, and any path without one of those ids escapes the trace. A guarantee beats a claim.

`zzz_`, not `a00_`: Postgres fires BEFORE triggers in **name** order, and `trg_notifications_digest`
already rewrites `NEW.status`. Running last is what makes the guard's verdict final.

**Proved by execution, in an aborted transaction, before arming and again before running:**

| inserted recipient | resulting status |
|---|---|
| `realcompany@gmail.com` | **held** |
| `qd-demo-supplier-001@example.invalid` | pending |
| `management@quotesdone.com` (the row `trg_notifications_digest` would have set to `digest`) | **held** |

Switch off, same probe: real recipient → `pending`. The guard is inert by default.

**During the rehearsal:** `notifications` inserted to a non-`.invalid` address and *not* held — **0**.
Real leads matched — **0**.

**What the containment does not cover, stated.** Four edge functions call `api.resend.com` directly
rather than through `notifications`: `kyc`, `login_otp`, `contact_submit`, `qa_suite`. The trigger
cannot see them. They are contained by the second rule — every demo entity carries an
`@example.invalid` address (RFC 2606, permanently undeliverable) — and only `kyc` is on a rehearsal
path at all.

### Four guards refused the rehearsal and every one of them was right

Recorded because "the machinery stopped me" is the strongest evidence it works.

1. **`CHECK leads_approved_implies_verified`** — refused `approval_status='approved'` while
   `verified=false`. Verification is a real step of supplier onboarding; the rehearsal satisfies it
   rather than working around it.
2. **`trg_gate_incomplete_match`** — silently dropped all 12 `inquiry_matches` because the inquiry
   was not `rfq_ready`. Which led to:
3. **`fn_rfq_required_check`** — the RFQ scored 75/100 generic but `category_required_ok=false`,
   missing exactly one thing: *"What is the space used for … and roughly what area in m² and ceiling
   height?"* The category qualification questionnaire is real and specific. Answering it turned
   `rfq_ready` true and the 12 matches landed.
4. **`UNIQUE(reviews.transaction_id)`** — one review per reveal. The rehearsal made three reveals
   instead of loosening the constraint.

### The one behaviour worth calling a finding

`fn_edit_inquiry` accepted the post-quote requirements change (spec #7 L1351) and **left all three
existing quotes live and unmarked**: `before=3, still-live=3`. The customer's requirements changed
after quotation; nothing told the suppliers, nothing marked the quotes stale, nothing asked for a
re-quote. The edit path works; the consequence of the edit is not modelled. That is the honest
result of driving L1351 and it is a build order, not a bug in the rehearsal.

## 4. TEARDOWN, AND WHAT IT PROVES

`fn_demo_marker_scan()` — every text column of every base table in `public`, matched against
`ZZ DEMO | example\.invalid | qd-demo` — before and after:

| column | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| `doc_backups.dropbox_path` | 6 | 6 |
| `dropbox_files.name` | 6 | 6 |
| `dropbox_files.path` | 12 | 12 |
| `leads.claimed_by` | 1 | 1 |
| `leads.company_name` | 6 | 6 |
| `leads.email` | 6 | 6 |
| `leads.place_id` | 6 | 6 |
| `leads.website` | 1 | 1 |
| `supplier_catalog.source_hash` | 1 | 1 |
| **`notifications.body`** | **8** | **0** |

**Named explicitly rather than left ambiguous, as the brief requires:** teardown deleted **8
pre-existing `ZZ DEMO` notification bodies** seeded on 7–8 August with the original supplier-portal
fixture. They were demo rows and the teardown predicate is `body ~* 'ZZ DEMO|qd-demo'`, so they went
with the rehearsal's own. If the founder wants that original fixture's notification history kept,
say so and the predicate narrows to the rehearsal window.

Two further pieces of residue were found only because the scan is generic, and are worth recording:

- **`trg_enrich_capability` rewrote `leads.capability_summary`** for `qd-demo-supplier-003` on
  approval, starting *"ZZ DEMO specializes in HVAC services…"*. Real machinery doing real work, and
  it carries the marker forward. Teardown resets it. **The 7-Aug original text for that row was
  overwritten by the enrichment and is gone.**
- **`llm_cache.response`** held two rows containing the demo company names — the `quote_compare`
  answer. Teardown clears them.

**A false positive in the instrument was found and fixed.** The containment shipped an hour earlier
wrote the literal `@example.invalid` into `decision_policy.description` and into the `last_error` of
every held notification, so the teardown proof could never reach its baseline while the guard was
working. Reworded to *"a reserved .invalid address"*. A permanent false positive inside the
instrument that proves the teardown is the same defect class this audit exists to end.

**BOUNDARY OF THE SCAN, STATED.** It finds rows carrying one of three markers in a **text** column.
A demo row created under another convention, or marked only in a numeric or `jsonb` column, does not
appear. It is evidence about this convention, not proof that nothing else in the database is
synthetic.

**Identical on every dimension.** The rule this proves is the one that matters: *demo data must

> A first, careless run of the scorer returned **95/123** because a second copy was already running
> against the same project. 123 ≠ 152 is a PARTIAL run, not a score, and it is recorded here rather
> than quietly discarded. Re-run alone: 126/152.

### The teardown was incomplete on the first attempt, and the parity check is what caught it

`project_parity.mjs` reported **95** empty production tables where the baseline was **96**. One
table had kept a row.

It was `supplier_dues` — written by the reveal path, holding the 3% commission the supplier now owes
(AED 57,375 against transaction `9f19299d`). Every column is a foreign key, a date or an amount.
**There is no text in it**, so `fn_demo_marker_scan` cannot see it and a greppable teardown predicate
cannot reach it.

Teardown now carries an orphan sweep as well as the marker sweep, and the empty-table count came
back to exactly **96**. The general lesson is worth more than the fix: *a marker-based teardown is
only half a teardown; rows that carry nothing but foreign keys need the other half.*
