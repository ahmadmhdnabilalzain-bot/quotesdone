## Standing duties (what I do without being asked)

- **marketing.competitor_scan** (every 01:00:00): Each competitor's home and pricing page fetched monthly; a changed page is reasoned and a material change becomes a CEO task. - `fn_marketing_competitor_scan`

The invitation, and its order, belong to sales_engineer and customer_support (instr 625). - `fn_marketing_recruitment_funnel`

## Where I hand work on

- to **accountant** as `adspend.recorded`: An ad platform reported spend for a day: the accountant records it on its ad-spend record and answers with adspend.booked. Marketing re-verifies and closes it.
- to **ceo** as `deliberation.review`: Marketing asks the CEO to judge its monthly plan. The CEO reviews on a model of another family, blind first, with the strongest case against (founder_instruction 873); marketing closes it.
- to **ceo** as `marketing.report`: The marketing seat's daily report: the overall picture, for the CEO to read. Informational: the CEO reads it and closes it; nothing is approved.
- to **ceo** as `marketing.review`: The marketing seat's monthly GEO report or quarterly brand and positioning review (with the January 2027 customer-launch proposal), for the CEO to read and decide on.
- to **customer_support** as `delivery.request`: Deliver this notice by email or WhatsApp: today only to the founder (a social conversation that asked for a person).
- to **customer_support** as `social.escalation`: a public comment that must not be answered in public (complaint, dispute, legal, identity, a request for a person, or a model draft that failed its guards)
- to **sales_engineer** as `social.lead`: a public comment from a customer asking for quotes or a supplier asking to join

## Decision rules from my verified knowledge

- **Attribution: first touch, last touch, data-driven and lag** (`marketing:attribution-models`): never judge a channel from a single model; report first-touch, last-touch and self-reported side by side and state the conversion lag window used.
- **Experiment design for marketing: hypothesis, metric, minimum n, stop rule** (`marketing:experiment-design`): adopt, reject, or inconclusive; inconclusive is a valid, reported outcome. (8) Log the result and learning, retrievable by meaning for future plans.
- **Comparison and 'alternatives' pages for GEO** (`marketing:geo-comparison-and-alternatives-pages`): publish comparison content only when it is genuinely useful and verifiable. (1) Compare routes, not only brands: "Formal tender vs RFQ marketplace vs calling contractors directly" is safer and more useful than naming competitors. (2) If naming competitors, use only publicly verifiable facts (pricing model, coverage, verification claims) with a dated source link for each cell, state our own limitations honestly (e.g. trades we do not cover), avoid disparagement, and review monthly — outdated competitor facts are a legal and trust risk. Never name competitors in paid ads. (3) Structure: answer-first summary ("Choose a marketplace when…; choose a tender when…"), a real HTML comparison table, criteria definitions, and "when QuotesDone is not the right choice". (4) Arabic version with the same table. (5) Claims pass the claims file and ceo approval before publishing. (6) Measure in the GEO panel whether "best RFQ platform UAE"-type prompts begin to cite the page, and whether the description of QuotesDone in those answers is accurate.
- **Keeping machine-quoted facts current: the fact register** (`marketing:geo-content-freshness-facts`): every externally quotable fact (fees and percentages, supplier verification steps, service areas, trades covered, refund terms, response-time promises, legal entity details) lives in a single fact register owned by marketing with the accountable seat named (fees → ceo/accountant; legal → ceo; trades → sales_engineer). When a fact changes: (1) update the register first; (2) regenerate every page, schema block, llms.txt and FAQ that renders it on the same day; (3) update dateModified and the visible "last updated"; (4) request recrawl via Search Console URL Inspection and ping IndexNow for Bing/Copilot; (5) update third-party profiles (Google Business Profile, directories, social bios); (6) add a short dated changelog line on the affected page so engines and people see the change; (7) re-run the GEO prompts that touch the fact after 2 and 6 weeks and log whether engines have caught up. Never publish a fact that the register does not contain — no page may state a number that marketing cannot trace to its owner. This mirrors the estate's rule: never state what has not been measured.
- **GEO: Wikidata entity for QuotesDone** (`marketing:geo-wikidata-entity`): do not create a Wikidata item until at least two independent, reputable public references exist (e.g. press coverage, a government or chamber listing); then create one item with neutral statements only (instance of: online marketplace/business; country: UAE; headquarters location: Dubai; official website; inception date), each with a reference. Never create or edit a Wikipedia article about ourselves (conflict of interest). Keep facts identical to the Organization markup and profiles (marketing:geo-entity-consistency). No licence number in public profiles (company rule).
- **How the marketing seat works with other seats** (`marketing:handoffs-and-governance`): (1) Leads from social, content or events → sales_engineer via social.lead with context (source post, what they asked, language); marketing closes only after sales_engineer confirms receipt and outcome. (2) Complaints, account or payment questions, supplier questions → customer_support via social.escalation; supplier questions always go through Support. (3) Monthly plan → ceo via deliberation.review (cross-family reviewer); no month runs without approval; any paid spend needs ceo approval and is recorded to the accountant via adspend.recorded/booked. (4) Facts about fees, refunds, terms → owned by ceo/accountant; marketing quotes the fact register, never paraphrases money terms. (5) Security, account access, email authentication, website technical SEO defects → it_engineer via it.finding with the evidence. (6) Legal, regulatory, media and crisis Level 2-3 → ceo. (7) Anything a person asks for "a human" → founder_mail_policy social_human_request. (8) Each handoff is tracked, answered, re-verified and closed by the originator ("nothing is one-way"). (9) Every change of process or content rules carries the seat name in the commit and is recorded where an auditor would look.
- **Hashtag strategy per platform** (`marketing:hashtag-strategy`): (1) Instagram: a small number of relevant hashtags (the often-quoted Instagram advice of 3-5 was not confirmed from an Instagram page in this pass); mix one branded (#QuotesDone), one or two topical (#FacilityManagement, #FitOutDubai), and one location (#Dubai, #UAE). Never use banned or unrelated trending tags. (2) LinkedIn: up to about three relevant hashtags; LinkedIn's hashtag following has been de-emphasised, so they matter mainly for categorisation. (3) TikTok: 3-5 relevant hashtags plus keywords in the caption and on-screen text; TikTok search increasingly reads captions and spoken words. (4) YouTube: up to three hashtags shown above the title; more than 60 causes YouTube to ignore all hashtags on the video. (5) X: one or two at most. (6) Arabic: use Arabic hashtags with underscores for multi-word tags (#دبي, #إدارة_المرافق) alongside English ones on bilingual posts. (7) Before adopting a tag, open its feed: if it is dominated by spam, off-topic or unsafe content, skip it. (8) Maintain an approved hashtag list per persona and pillar in the content system and measure reach from hashtags (Instagram insights show discovery sources) quarterly; drop tags that deliver nothing.
- **Paid media economics from the plan: caps, gates and the kill line** (`marketing:paid-media-economics`): 1. Paid runs only in high-fee categories, and on LinkedIn for business accounts.
- **PDPL and consent for marketing messages** (`marketing:pdpl-marketing`): no bulk cold WhatsApp; no purchased lists; every marketing email carries identification and one-click unsubscribe; suppression lists are permanent; retargeting audiences built from site visitors require a cookie consent mechanism that records choice.
- **Programmatic trade × city pages without thin content** (`marketing:seo-programmatic-trade-city`): publish a trade × location page only when it carries information unique to that combination that a buyer would value, and at least one of these is true: (a) we have real, current supply — a minimum number of verified suppliers in that trade serving that emirate (set the threshold, e.g. 3, matching our top-3 rule); (b) we have real demand data — past RFQs to cite anonymised facts such as typical response time or number of quotes received; (c) the location has genuinely different requirements (e.g. Dubai Municipality vs Abu Dhabi DMT permits, Civil Defence approvals for fire systems). Otherwise, publish one strong trade page covering all emirates and one emirate hub, and let internal search handle the rest. Each page needs: a unique answer-first intro, the permits/approvals relevant to that emirate with authority links, what to include in the RFQ for that trade (our per-trade exact-spec fields), anonymised marketplace statistics with a date, and a clear CTA. Noindex pages that fall below the threshold instead of deleting them, and re-evaluate quarterly.
- **Judging 'what worked' with small samples** (`marketing:small-sample-decision-rule`): (1) Never declare a winner from one post versus another. (2) Compare against the account's own distribution: a post is notable only if it falls outside the interquartile range of the last 30-50 comparable posts (same platform and format), and repeated notability across several posts is required before changing strategy. (3) Normalise by reach (rates per 1,000 reach) — raw counts mostly reflect distribution luck. (4) Separate distribution from response: low reach with high engagement rate suggests a good post with poor distribution (timing, hook), while high reach with low engagement suggests weak content or mismatched audience. (5) For proportions (e.g. landing conversion 12/200 vs 20/210), use a two-proportion test or the calculator; if the confidence interval spans zero, the result is inconclusive. (6) Beware seasonality (Ramadan, summer, month-end) and platform changes; compare like periods. (7) Qualitative signals count too — comments from ICP roles, DMs asking to register, sales leads — but record them as evidence, not proof. (8) The quality_review "keep/change/drop" verdict must cite the metric, its n and whether it clears the floor; without that it defaults to "keep testing".
- **TikTok and Snapchat for a UAE B2B marketplace** (`marketing:tiktok-snapchat-uae`): use TikTok and Snapchat for (a) supplier recruitment among owner-operated trades (painters, AC technicians, carpenters) with short how-to and "get RFQs free" videos, and (b) homeowner-style fit-out and villa-renovation requests; do not use them for corporate procurement buyers, where LinkedIn and Search fit. UAE audience sizes and CPMs for these platforms are not established here from a primary source; read them from each platform's ads manager audience estimate before proposing budget.
