# QuotesDone — Executive Competency Audit
**Independent organizational assessment of the AI executive team**
Assessor: external executive recruiter / organizational assessor (Fortune-500 standard)
Date: 24 July 2026 · Scope: the AI organization in `suppliers DB` (org_departments, org_agents, agent_knowledge, dept_library, decision_policy, org_authority, governance) — **not** the code, UI, or workflows.
Benchmark: a score of 95+ = an executive who could lead that function in a mature international company. Scores are deliberately not inflated.

## 0. Method

Each department was treated as a candidate for a senior executive role. The "interview" tested what the department can actually bring to a decision: its own knowledge base, the shared canon it retrieves, the policies it must obey, the tools/authority it holds (org_authority + action executors), and its recent work product (org_memory deliverables, exec_reports, canon_eval results). Questions escalated from fundamentals → strategy → crisis scenarios → cross-department conflicts, until the candidate's limit was found. Every claim below cites evidence from the database.

## 1. The headline finding (cross-department, critical)

**The organization does not agree on its own money model.** The canonical, auto-synced pricing knowledge (`Pricing & fees (LIVE)`, `canon:money`, policy_rules #1–#6) states: buyers pay a **3% reveal fee**; suppliers pay **no commission**; supplier registration is free until 31 July 2027 (first 1,000 registrants permanently exempt), then AED 1,000/yr; the only supplier dues are the **non-response recovery** (equal to the refunded reveal fee) and the AED 10,000 circumvention sum; late-payment interest per the signed Supplier Agreement is **9% per annum simple**, capped at principal.

Against that canon, at interview:

- **Collections** described its core job as "track the 2% commission owed by suppliers on won jobs, due within 14 working days… applying 1%/month + AED 250" — a fee that does not exist, at an interest basis (monthly) the signed agreement forbids. Its own text matches the company's *forbidden-language regex* (policy_rules #1, severity critical).
- **Bookkeeper** books "supplier commissions as receivable at reveal… clear on collection within 14 working days."
- **CFO** stated revenue as "reveal fees (2%, uncapped)" — wrong rate — and tracks a "supplier commission collection rate."
- **Legal**'s standing debt-recovery ladder is built for "unpaid commission" and quotes "max 12% p.a." interest, where decision_policy explicitly rules the signed agreement governs at 9% p.a. Legal's ladder also includes the AED 250 admin fee without the caveat (recorded in decision_policy) that it is **not yet contractually recoverable**.
- **Chief of Staff**'s daily-brief spec still reports "overdue commissions."
- The global "Penalty & collections model" row itself says "AED 100/year" registration (typo for 1,000) and "max 12% p.a."

**Business risk:** a demand letter or dunning email citing a nonexistent commission at a wrong interest basis is (a) legally unfounded, (b) a breach of the company's own forbidden-language policy, (c) reputationally damaging with the exact suppliers the company must court pre-launch. This is the single fastest way for an autonomous finance/legal stack to create real-world liability. No Fortune-500 CFO/GC pair would survive this interview question.

Secondary cross-department contradictions found: Document Control's reminder policy ("~7 days before expiry, not earlier" — a deliberate founder rule) vs. its own deepened playbook and Onboarding's playbook (both say 60/30/7 days); and three different registration-fee phrasings across rows.

## 3. Cross-department evaluation

**Tested pairs and what they revealed:**

- **Finance + Legal (supplier default scenario):** *FAIL.* Collections would compute a debt Legal would demand at a different rate for a fee neither should be chasing. The two departments would jointly produce an unlawful demand letter. Root cause: knowledge rows written before the pricing pivot were never reconciled against policy_rules — and the forbidden-language linter apparently never scans `agent_knowledge` itself.
- **IT + Security (breach scenario):** *PARTIAL.* Both have strong halves (IT: regulator clocks, continuity; Security: PICERL, containment instincts) but no joint runbook, no incident commander, and the notification duties cited are largely inapplicable regimes (DESC/DIFC/ADGM) while the applicable one (PDPL/Data Office) is unowned.
- **Marketing + Analytics (campaign scenario):** *ONE-SIDED.* Marketing's tracking-gate discipline is world-class, but there is no Analytics counterpart to define conversion metrics or arbitrate attribution. Marketing currently self-referees.
- **Customer Success + Procurement (bad-supplier scenario):** *PARTIAL.* The refund flow is well-policied (working-day windows, tri-party notice), but CS lacks the complaint/dispute layer and Procurement lacks an owner to answer for repeat-offender suppliers beyond auto-blocking.
- **Supplier Success + Operations (surge scenario):** *PARTIAL.* Invite ramp is deliverability-gated (good) but nothing connects supplier-acquisition targeting to category-level demand gaps except one-off analytics tasks.
- **Chief of Staff coordination:** *PASS with limits.* The objective→tasks decomposition demonstrably works across 6 departments; what's missing is the conflict tie-break and crisis command noted in §2.1.

**Verdict:** the organization behaves like one company for *routine, planned* work (objectives, briefs, gates, audit trails are genuinely coherent), and like isolated specialists in *contradiction and crisis* — precisely the two conditions where an autonomous company is most exposed.
